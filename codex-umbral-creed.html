Part I

The Umbral Creed

The Word Bearers taught the galaxy that faith is a weapon. The Umbral Creed is what happens when a chapter of that Legion reads its own scripture too closely.

They were a preaching company like ten thousand others: crimson armor, silver trim, portable reliquaries, a schedule. They burned what they were told to burn and illuminated the margins of the Book of Lorgar by lamplight afterward. The company's Master of Possessions, a patient man named Sor Vekh, noticed the same defect in every summoning he performed: the daemon arrives, the daemon obeys, and behind the daemon's eyes something older watches the transaction and finds it funny.

Vekh did what scholars do. He footnoted it.

Forty years of footnotes became a concordance, and the concordance became a heresy within the heresy: the four gods are loud, and the thing beneath them is not. Prayer addressed to the silence gets answered more precisely. The company tested this the way the Word Bearers test everything, on a world that could not refuse, and the answer that came back had wings, a blade, and the oldest name in the ledger of the damned.

Be'lakor did not demand worship. He corrected their pronunciation.

They repainted nothing. The crimson stays, because the Creed holds that they are still Word Bearers, still faithful, still reading the same book. They have simply reached a later chapter. What changed is the congregation: horrors that chant in colors, hounds that answer the tithe, nurglings that annotate the footnotes by chewing them, and above the whole procession the First Prince, who finds everything the gods built derivative and says so.

The Creed's doctrine, in one line: the gods are quotations. Serve the source.

The Blood Tithe

A congregation that worships silence still has to pay its bills, and the coin of Chaos is blood. In the high summer of the Creed's second year the tithe came due, and the court of Khorne arrived to collect it in person: a Bloodthirster, unbound and unassembled, patient in his crate; a Herald with nine sworn killers; five hounds of flesh who can hear a lie at nine inches and take it personally.

The Creed does not love Khorne. The Creed pays him, promptly, the way you pay any collector you intend to keep using. Khorne does not care from whence the blood flows. Lately it has flowed mostly from California.

Part II

Armoury of Shadow

The two rules engines the whole army runs on, condensed. The repo's belakor-shadow-legion-guide.md holds the full detail; the official app is the arbiter of exact numbers.

This is the July digest — the later-verified rules gists live in data/muster.json and on Muster's rules check; where they differ (Chaos Terminators on the Thralls list, the Tzeentch and Slaanesh boon wording, the six verified stratagems, Deep Strike at more than 8″), the store wins.

Shadow of Chaos — the army rule

The battlefield divides into in shadow and not. Your zone starts in shadow; it spreads with map control (hold at least half the objectives in the enemy deployment zone at the start of a phase and their zone joins yours for that phase), and Be'lakor and the Greater Daemons carry it with them in a 6" bubble.

Daemonic Manifestation. Friendly daemons in shadow add 1 to Battle-shock tests; a passed test heals D3 wounds, or returns up to D3 destroyed models to a Battleline unit. The Horrors are the reason this line matters.

Daemonic Terror. Enemy units in shadow, or near a Greater Daemon, subtract 1 from Battle-shock tests and take D3 mortal wounds on a failure. Battle-shocked units drop to OC 0 and stop holding objectives.

The army's true win condition: push the shadow forward, make your holders unkillable, make theirs stop counting.

Shadow Legion — the detachment (2 DP · Purge the Foe)

Be'lakor's own legion: every god's daemons plus mortal Chaos Space Marines under one banner. Be'lakor and all Heretic Astartes gain Shadow Legion and Undivided; all Legiones Daemonica gain Shadow Legion.

The mortal half. Up to half the army from a fixed CSM allowlist (named Thralls of the First Prince): Chaos Lord (foot, Terminator, jump pack), Sorcerer (foot, Terminator), Master of Possessions, Dark Apostle, Legionaries, Chosen, Possessed, Raptors, Warp Talons, Havocs, and Damned units. Heretic Astartes here gain Deep Strike.

The fine print. Be'lakor must be your Warlord when present (Supreme Commander is mandatory). No vehicles — the War Dogs attend via the Chaos Knights Dreadblades rule (max 3 in an all-Chaos army; no Warlord, no enhancements). No Daemon Princes. No Epic Heroes except Be'lakor — Skulltaker and Karanak are specifically illegal.

Known toolkit. Warp Surge (1 CP: a daemon unit in shadow charges after Advancing) · Denizens of the Warp (1 CP: deep strike at 3" instead of 9") · Binding Shadow (pull an endangered unit into reserves — the rescue half of the yo-yo) · Leaping Shadows (enhancement: Scouts 9") · Fade to Darkness (enhancement: the bearer's unit kills, vanishes from the board, and returns next turn — the detachment's signature trick, legal on a Bloodthirster). One stratagem per unit per phase this edition, so spend the command point on the moment that matters.

Part III

The Hosts of the Creed

The Dark Master

Be'lakor The Source

390 pts

First of the Daemon Princes, author of the silence the Creed reads.

On the table he is a moving cathedral: tough (T11, 3+, 20 wounds, invulnerable save, −1 to be hit, no re-rolls against him, untargetable at long range), decently violent (Devastating Wounds on the strike, Sustained Hits on the sweep), and carrying three auras that are the actual purchase — the shadow itself, re-roll 1s to hit for the faithful, and a 9" terror bubble that heals him D3 every time an enemy unit fails its nerve nearby.

On the battlefield. He is the umbrella, and everything fragile stands under him. Fly him behind terrain turns one and two, or deep strike him with the second wave. His damage is real but his job is position: wherever he stands, the army rule follows and the enemy's Leadership starts costing them mortal wounds. Do not spend him like an assassin; spend him like weather. Bring him and he is your Warlord whether you like it or not; the Creed likes it.

Part IV

Ways of War

Three doctrines, one army. Pick by opponent.

The Long Shadow vs. shooting armies

Start thin: Nurglings on the midfield objectives, Havocs home, Horrors behind terrain, everything else in reserve as the rules allow (check current reserve caps before writing it on a list). Turns one and two the opponent shoots giggles and terrain. Turns two and three the sky opens: Be'lakor, the Anagnost, and the Possessed arrive together on the weak flank, Denizens of the Warp tightens a charge if one must land, and the shadow arrives with them. Mallex's squad runs the yo-yo underneath it all — kill, Fade, return. You were never not there; you were just late on purpose.

The Crimson Tithe vs. armies that want time

The new wing inverts the tempo. Leaping Shadows on the Bloodthirster, hounds and letters forward, everything Khorne Advancing and still charging. The tithe hits the midfield on turn one or two before a castle finishes forming, and the rest of the codex walks in behind it. Riskier, louder, and the reason the Blood Tithe was purchased.

The Patient Litany vs. melee armies

Castle. Horrors screen, monsters counterpunch, the Manifestation engine resurrects the line while Terror and Be'lakor's aura tax every Leadership check the opponent makes near an objective. Battle-shock the contesters, never the champions; a flipped objective at the right moment beats a dead Terminator squad.

Musters

The Quartermaster built six; these are the three that matter now. Full tables for all six live in quartermaster.md and the ledger artifact.

The Yo-Yo Court — 1,940 pts, COMPLETE as of August 4. Be'lakor · Lord of Change · Mallex + Fade → 5 Legionaries · 5 Legionaries · MoP → Possessed · Havocs · Fateskimmer → Screamers · Exalted Flamer → Flamers · 10 Pinks · Nurglings · 5 Flesh Hounds · Cultists. Teleport control: the Tzeentch castle holds under the boons while Mallex's squad kills, Fades, and returns. Every model painted. This is the list to learn on, starting today.

Triple Monster — 2,000 pts, needs only assembly. Be'lakor · Bloodthirster (Fade) · Lord of Change · 10 Pinks · 10 Blues · Nurglings · Fateskimmer → Screamers · Havocs · 5 Legionaries · MoP → Possessed · Cultists. Three T10–11 monsters each dragging a 6" shadow bubble own most of the board by turn two. Beginner-forgiving. Unlocked the moment the Unbound Word gets his arms.

The Fadethirster — the tournament shape. Adds Skullmaster + 6 Bloodcrushers and a Rendmaster on Blood Throne to the Bloodthirster loop; every 4-1 result the Battle Doctrine deck studied runs this silhouette, usually pairing in the Cavalcade of Chaos detachment. The inbound Bloodcrushers lot decides how much of it you already own; a Skullmaster and Rendmaster are the true remaining gaps.
