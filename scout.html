How this was made, and what it cannot tell you. Five research passes (~180 searches) over US discount retailers, Games Workshop's own store, eBay, the Walmart/Amazon marketplaces and US second-hand shops. A price appears here only if it literally appeared in a search result; where the index showed a price without tying it to an item number, the row says "unattributed" and links to the search, never to a guessed listing. No photo was seen and no listing was confirmed live — every eBay number below is a lead to open, not a verdict. Two things changed the maths since July: the vision now prefers bare or new-on-sprue over painted (the shadow-first scheme is quicker to paint than to retrofit), and US import rules: the de minimis exemption is suspended for every country (made permanent regulation on 2026-06-24) and a baseline tariff applies, collected by the carrier with a brokerage fee on delivery — so a UK, EU, Canadian or Ukrainian listing that looks $10 cheaper is not. Everything recommended here is US-located. "Landed" keeps July's convention (price × 1.03 + shipping; sales tax not included).

What moved in the market since July

  • Prices only went up, a little. GW raised US prices ≈ 4% on 2025-10-06 and expects more tariff cost this year; kit tags seen in this scan: Chaos Terminators and Chosen $62.50 → $65, Combat Patrols $160 → $170, battleforces to $255. US discounters still take 15% off as standard (Game Nerdz, Miniature Market, Frontline, Flipside, most independents), with free shipping from $75 at Game Nerdz ($6 flat below) and Miniature Market ($6.99 below) — so pair purchases.
  • Three of the needed kits are GW web-exclusives — Blood Throne/Skull Cannon ($48), Skullmaster ($52, resin), Plague Drones ($65): independents carry a couple at near-full price or none, which is why eBay new-in-box is where the discount is.
  • Bloodcrushers are now sold only as a box of six ($110; $99 at Wargame Portal). Three-model lots persist on eBay at ≈ $54–60 new-on-sprue (one US seller does 2 for $57 each, 3 for $54 each). Irrelevant until the crate is opened.
  • Raptors/Warp Talons: new kit (first in Kill Team: Shadowhunt, Feb 2026; standalone from Aug 8 at $65). The January faction-pack update also gave Warp Talons the GRENADES keyword — re-read the datasheet in the app when C or D gets close.
  • Combat Patrol: Chaos Daemons is 34 models (not the 19 sometimes quoted), still $170, and drifting out of stock (Noble Knight lists it "out of print"; several stores backordered). Chaos Daemons stay an index faction in 11th edition with three detachments in the current pack; no kit retirements or new patrol box are announced — buying needed singles now is low-risk, and waiting gains nothing known.
  • Coupon sites report an eBay 20%-off code (BRANDS20) for Aug 10–16 on "eligible items"; it is real only where the listing page shows the banner. A bonus if it appears on the Blood Throne listing, not a plan.

How to check a lead in two minutes (photos, not titles)

  1. Location line first. "Ships from United States" or walk away — GBP/EUR/CAD prices, "$0 US tariffs" boasts and "$1.22 standard shipping from outside US" all mean duty and brokerage on your doorstep.
  2. Count against the box. Blood Throne/Skull Cannon: three sprues (or a sealed box) and the large oval base. Bloodcrushers: three riders, three Juggernauts, three oval bases per old box. Plague Drones: three flies, three Plaguebearer riders, flying stems. Flesh Hounds: five hounds, five bases — a $26 "squad" is often three. Nurglings: three swarm bases. Terminators/Chosen/Warp Talons: five bodies, five bases, and for Talons paired lightning claws and no guns (pistols and chainswords make them Raptors). Skullmaster: grey/cream GW resin, 17 parts, a 60 mm round base — bendy, bubbly or bright-white resin at half price is a recast.
  3. Built is fine, built wrong is not. A glued Skull Cannon (gun carriage, two crew) is not a Blood Throne (herald up top on a stepped dais); Dark Vengeance Chosen are six fixed-pose 2012 models, legal but option-less; Cataphractii/Tartaros (Horus Heresy) and Scarab Occult are not Chaos Terminators; square bases mean old Warhammer Fantasy stock to re-base.
  4. Seller and format. 99%+ feedback with volume; "80 sold" store listings are the safest new stock; a current bid is not a price — decide your ceiling from the table above before opening an auction; "or best offer" on a $50 Beast invites $44.
  5. Stack the boring savings. Two kits in one Game Nerdz / Miniature Market order clear the $75 free-shipping bar; check the listing page for an eBay coupon banner; Warpfire and Frontline combine used lots with new kits in one parcel.

Method and limits

Search-index only (WebSearch from a cloud session whose network policy blocks eBay and every retailer; tools/ebay_search.js still needs a machine that can reach eBay). Five parallel research passes — Khorne kits, Nurgle kits, Chaos Space Marine kits, bundles and market context, US second-hand stores — plus verification searches on the strongest item numbers; about 180 queries. Prices are quoted only where the result text carried them (Shopify shops leak "regular/sale price", Walmart and Amazon snippets carry marketplace prices that go stale, eBay item pages rarely show price but hub pages do without item numbers). Stock levels are mostly unknown. Where shipping was not stated a $6 US-webstore guess is included and marked in the data. Sales tax is excluded, as in July. The JSON keeps every row's source, the quoted fragment and the URL so the next session — or Evan on his phone — can audit any number in under a minute.
