Practical consequence: you have three "storage classes" — (A) three huge winged centrepieces needing 8-11 in of headroom, (B) ~8-10 medium/tall pieces (War Dogs, chariot, drones, Beasts, Bloodcrushers, Daemon Prince) needing 4-7 in, and (C) ~60 infantry needing 2-2.5 in. No single off-the-shelf case does all three well; every good answer below is "one tall rigid box for class A (+B), one or two shallow boxes/case for class C".

## 1. The magnetised-storage approach in detail

### 1.1 Why magnets (and not foam) for THIS army
- Painted, spiky, second-hand Chaos models are the worst case for foam: foam rubs edges/horns and snags spikes, banners, staff tips and wing membranes; pluck-foam cavities big enough for a Lord of Change waste a whole case. Magnetised bases on steel let every model stand in free air touching nothing [verified discussion of the trade-offs: https://spikeybits.com/40k-miniatures-carrying-cases-storage-solutions-guide/ , https://www.tabletopbattles.com/hobby-101-transporting-your-models/ ].
- Foam still has a role for the genuinely fragile flyers (Screamers, drones, chariot) and for air travel — see sections 3-4.

### 1.2 How GW bases are built underneath (drives magnet choice)
- 25-50 mm Citadel round bases are hollow with a shallow plain recess. Magnet Baron sizes its "flush-fit" base magnets at **2.2 mm tall for 25/28.5 mm bases and 2.6 mm tall for 32 mm-and-up**, explicitly "engineered to match standard GW base depths" so the magnet face ends flush with the rim [verified: https://themagnetbaron.com/products/gw-flush-fit-base-magnets-large , https://themagnetbaron.com/blogs/news/base-magnetization-ultimate-guide]. Scarhandpainting's tutorial likewise says magnets **up to 2 mm tall fit under a GW base with just glue; thinner ones need green stuff packing** [verified: https://scarhandpainting.com/tutorial-magnetizing-gw-bases/].
- Take-away: a generic **2 mm-thick** disc glued straight to the underside sits a fraction of a millimetre shy of the steel (works, slightly weaker); a **3 mm-thick** disc will usually protrude on 25/32 mm bases and make the model rock — so either buy 2 mm-thick and bed them on a putty pad so the face is exactly flush, or buy Magnet Baron flush-fits. **[the exact recess depth varies by base size/mould — press a magnet in and check with a steel ruler before committing; uncited]**
- 60 mm+, ovals and 100/130/160 mm bases have deeper undersides with reinforcing ribs **[uncited — check yours]**: you build a putty "plinth" (or stack two magnets) so the magnet face reaches the rim plane, placing magnets between ribs near the rim. Magnet Baron's alternative for 100 mm+ is **three 25 mm peel-and-stick discs in a triangle "to resist tipping"** [verified: https://themagnetbaron.com/blogs/magnet-guide/warhammer-40k-base-size-chart].
- Flat-bottomed bases (old WFB squares, some resin/3D-printed bases, MDF): drill a shallow 5-6 mm pocket with a pin vice/drill bit and glue the magnet flush, or glue a magnet on top of a thin steel/putty pad and accept a 2 mm lift [verified how-to for resin/plastic: https://spikeybits.com/how-to-magnetize-resin-plastic-bases-for-miniatures/].

### 1.3 Magnet size / count per base (N52 neodymium discs)

LITKO — 3 x 2 mm for infantry up to 32 mm, 5-6 x 2 mm for 40 mm+ elites/monsters, "several spaced magnets on big bases" [verified: https://litko.net/pages/magnets-for-miniatures-guide]. Rough pull numbers for intuition **[uncited]**: a 6 x 2 N52 on bare steel holds ~0.5-0.7 kg in direct pull; a 10 x 3 ~2 kg. Shear/tilt resistance is far lower than direct pull, which is why big models get 3-4 magnets spread to the rim and why real steel (not ferrous rubber) matters for them.

### 1.4 Glue, putty, polarity, tools
- **Adhesive**: roughen the magnet face with sandpaper/file, then medium CA (superglue). CA alone on smooth nickel plating can pop off in cold or with shock, so bed each magnet in a pea of putty — green stuff (Kneadatite), Milliput, or Apoxie — pressed into the recess, magnet pushed in flush, thin CA wicked around it. The cheap instant alternative is **CA + baking soda** sprinkled around the magnet as a filler (sets rock hard in seconds). For the 10 x 3 magnets on the monsters/War Dogs use **5-minute two-part epoxy or JB Weld** plus a putty surround. **[uncited standard practice; the Spikeybits and Scarhandpainting tutorials linked above show the putty method]**
- **The flush trick**: lay cling film/baking paper over your steel sheet, put the magnets on the sheet (they self-seat flat), put putty + a drop of CA in each base recess, and press the model down onto its magnet(s). Everything cures perfectly coplanar with the rim, and polarity is automatically consistent if you loaded the magnets onto the sheet from one reference stack.
- **Polarity discipline**: steel doesn't care about polarity, but you will care later (magnet-lined movement trays, stacking spare magnets, models attracting/repelling neighbours when packed base-to-base, weapon swaps). Keep one "master stack"; mark its top with a Sharpie; every base magnet goes in with the marked face the same way. Magnet Baron sells polarity-keyed applicators ("Magnicators") in its starter bundle [verified product exists: https://themagnetbaron.com/products/magnetization-starter-pack].
- **Tools**: for hollow GW bases you need no drill at all. A pin vice with **1 mm / 2 mm / 3 mm** bits covers pinning flight stands and wing joints; a **5 mm and 6 mm** bit (or 13/64 in and 1/4 in) only if you sink magnets into resin/flat bases or into wing roots. Plus: hobby knife, sandpaper, nitrile gloves (CA + magnets + fingers is a bad trio), tin snips if cutting steel. **[uncited]**
- **Safety**: N52 discs shatter if allowed to snap together; keep them away from kids/pets (swallowing hazard) and from credit cards/mechanical watches.
- **Cost**: at bulk N52 prices (below) infantry costs **~$0.10-0.20 per model**, big models **~$1-2 each**; the whole ~75-model collection is **~$45-70 in magnets** with plenty spare.

## 3. Handling the awkward pieces

### 3.1 Magnetising wings on an already-built, painted LoC / Bloodthirster / Be'lakor — worth it?
**Usually no.** [uncited — reasoning + common experience]
- On all three kits the wings mount on large tabs into deep sockets in the back. If the previous owner used plastic cement the joint is welded — separating it means a razor saw through painted plastic, re-sculpting, repainting, and a real risk of cracking feathers/membranes. The payoff is small because a magnetised base in a tall box already solves transport, and un-magnetised wings are also sturdier on the table.
- **When it IS worth doing**: (a) a wing joint is already loose, gappy or was superglued (common on second-hand builds — CA joints often "tick" when flexed); (b) the model must fly in luggage regularly; (c) it arrives broken anyway. CA joints can often be parted cleanly after an hour in the freezer (CA goes brittle) or with CA debonder applied sparingly (it dulls paint — test underneath).
- **How, if you do it**: clean both faces; drill matched pockets for **two magnets per wing (6 x 3 or 8 x 3 mm N52; K&J D42-N52/D62-N52 class)** plus one **1.5-2 mm brass locating pin** so the wing cannot rotate/shear on the magnets; mind polarity per side; set magnets in epoxy, fill with green stuff, retexture, repaint the seam. Store detached wings in a foam slot or on their own small steel plate via a magnet glued to the tab. Budget 2-3 hours per model plus paint touch-up.
- **The better alternative for tall winged models**: leave them assembled, give the base 3-4 x 10 x 3 mm magnets on real steel, add "base capture" (3.3), and buy the box height. Be'lakor at 170 mm is a non-issue; only the Bloodthirster pushes box choice.

### 3.2 Flight-stand models (Screamers, Plague Drones, Burning Chariot)
Problems: GW clear stems are brittle polystyrene; the thin peg into the model and the X-slot into the base are the failure points; second-hand ones are often already re-glued with fogged CA. Options, from least to most work **[uncited standard practice]**:
1. **Triage tonight**: wiggle each stem. Anything that moves gets re-seated: drill 1-1.5 mm through base slot into the stem foot and pin with brass rod + CA; at the top, pin the peg into the model (drones especially — the body is heavy for that peg).
2. **Make the model detachable from the stem**: glue a 5 x 2 or 6 x 2 magnet into the model's socket and a matching magnet/steel screw head on the stem top (or use the Screamers' ball-socket dry, unglued, if it grips). For transport the bodies come off and lie flat in a shallow foam tray; bases + stems stay magnetised to the steel. This is the single most effective protection for Screamers and drones.
3. **Replace stems**: clear **acrylic rod 3/16 in (~5 mm) for drones/chariot, 1/8 in (3 mm) for Screamers** (TAP Plastics, Amazon, ~$6-12 for a several-foot length), or **2-3 mm brass rod** painted black — drill both ends 5-8 mm deep, pin and epoxy. Acrylic rod is far tougher than the GW stem and still "invisible"; brass is indestructible but visible.
4. **Burning Chariot specifically**: it is a 120 x 92 mm oval carrying a tall, springy flame stand with the disc, Screamers and reins hanging off it. Give the base **4x 10 x 3 mm** magnets, pin the flame stand into the base and into the disc with 2 mm brass, and either (a) transport it upright in the tall box with base capture (3.3) and nothing within 3 cm of it, or (b) give it its own **9 L XL / 19 L RUB** with two foam blocks notched to grip only the base rim. If the chariot-to-flames joint is already weak, magnetise *that* joint (2x 6 x 3 + pin) so the chariot body travels lying in foam and the flames/base travel upright.
5. Keep one small **pluck-foam or KR/Feldherr half-tray (~2 in deep)** as the "flyer ward" inside an infantry RUB: Screamer bodies, drone bodies, the Changeling's staff-type characters, anything mid-repair.

### 3.3 War Dogs and other top-heavy pieces — magnets plus a brace
- War Dogs: 100 mm round base, tall narrow legs, heavy torso/carapace weapons = high centre of gravity. Use **3x 10 x 3 mm N52 in a wide triangle at the rim** (Magnet Baron's guide for Armigers/War Dogs covers weapon and waist magnets too: https://themagnetbaron.com/blogs/imperial-knights/armiger-war-dog-magnetization-recommendations [verified page]) on **real steel**, not ferrous rubber.
- **Base capture brace** [uncited, widely used for Knights]: stick two or three small blocks (EVA foam, cork, or 3D-printed clips) to the steel with VHB so they kiss the *base rim* on the sides facing the direction of travel; the magnets stop lift, the blocks stop slide/tilt, and nothing touches the model. Removable putty-tack blocks work too. For absolute security on the two War Dogs and the Bloodthirster you can go mechanical: an M4 nylon bolt + wing nut through a hole drilled in the base and the box floor (invisible under texture paint/plug), which is how many Knight players travel.
- If the waist/weapon joints are unglued press-fits (common on War Dogs built for weapon swaps), either magnetise them properly (6 x 2 / 8 x 3 pairs per the guide above) or pop the torsos off for transport and stand them on their own magnets/foam — a loose torso is what actually falls off in a braking event.
- Orientation: put tall models near the middle of the floor, long base axis pointing fore-aft in the car, >= 3 cm from walls and from each other's wing tips.

### 3.4 Movement trays that double as storage trays
- Magnet-ready movement trays (5x 32 mm, 10x 32 mm, 5x 40 mm, 3x 60 mm etc.) with a **steel or ferrous insert in each socket, and magnets or steel tape underneath the tray**: squads live on their trays permanently; the whole tray lifts from the RUB to the table and back. Deployment and pack-up of 60 daemons drops from 15 minutes to 3. Sources: LITKO (acrylic trays + "FlexSteel" bottoms), Green Stuff World MDF trays + self-adhesive rubber-steel sheet, countless 3D-print/Etsy sellers ("magnetic movement tray 32mm") **[uncited: ~$5-15 per tray; you would want ~8-10 trays ≈ $60-120]**.
- Caveat: model-magnet-to-ferrous-insert is a weak link; for car travel it is fine for plastic infantry, but for anything metal or tall keep model magnets on real steel.

## 4. Travel

### 4.1 Car [uncited — general knowledge]
- **Placement**: footwell behind the front seats (lowest, coolest, cannot fall) or on the seat with the seatbelt through the RUB handles; non-slip shelf liner under boxes in a trunk/hatch; heavy tall box at the bottom of any stack; never loose on a seat edge.
- **Sliding inside the box**: real steel + N52 at the sizes above will hold plastic models through hard braking; failures come from ferrous-rubber sheet, single small magnets under tall models, magnets not flush (air gap), or unbraced top-heavy walkers. Do the "tilt test" at home: loaded box tipped to 45-60 degrees each way, then a firm shake.
- **Heat**: a closed car in summer sun commonly reaches 55-70 °C inside, hotter on the dash/parcel shelf, and clear RUBs add their own greenhouse. Consequences: resin (Forge World/Finecast) and 3D-printed parts soften and droop from ~50-60 °C; PVA/tufts/basing let go; CA joints embrittle with heat-and-humidity cycling; varnish can turn tacky; **standard N-grade neodymium (incl. most N52) is only rated to ~80 °C and loses strength permanently if taken above its max operating temperature**. Polystyrene GW plastic itself tolerates more (~90 °C+) but by then everything glued to it has failed. Rule: models ride in the cabin with you and come inside at every stop; never the trunk on a hot day, never overnight in the car. Throw an old towel over clear boxes in sun.
- **Cold**: CA gets brittle well below freezing — a winter trunk plus a pothole is how superglued wings come off. Same rule: cabin, not trunk.
- **Humidity**: store at home in conditioned space, not garage/attic; toss a few silica-gel packs in each RUB (they are near-airtight); high humidity while gluing causes CA "frosting" on paint — glue magnets in a dry room and let CA cure 12-24 h before closing lids (CA fumes fog clear parts and can bloom on fresh paint in a sealed box).

### 4.2 Flying with the army [uncited — general knowledge; check your airline and tsa.gov before you fly]
- **Sizes**: US majors' carry-on limit is typically **22 x 14 x 9 in (56 x 36 x 23 cm)** including wheels/handles; personal item roughly **17-18 x 13-14 x 8-9 in** (varies by airline; basic-economy fares may exclude a carry-on). None of the tall RUBs qualify: 32 L US is ~19 x 14 x 12.25 in external, 19 L XL ~15.5 x 10 x 13 in (the 10 in side busts the 9 in limit), 64 L is far too big. The Battle Foam P.A.C.K. 432/Go and Tablewar Mini are built around cabin dimensions; A-Case sells a "DuoPack – Ultimate Airplane Traveler" set for exactly this [exists, verified: https://shop.army-case.com/miniature-carrying-case/duopack-ultimate-airplane-traveler/ ; specs not captured].
- **The greater daemons on a plane**: standing upright they need ~10-11 in of height inside a bag whose smallest dimension may be 9 in — it only works if the rigid inner box is loaded so its height runs along the bag's 22 in axis and its footprint is within ~13.5 x 8.5 in: i.e. **one daemon per carry-on-legal box, carried upright, never in the overhead on its side**. Realistically: (a) drive to events with the centrepieces; or (b) fly them **lying on their backs in model-specific foam (Feldherr LoC 140 mm / Bloodthirster 130 mm trays) inside a hard case** as your carry-on, with the magnetised infantry in a personal-item-size box or Magna Rack bag; or (c) magnetise the wings (3.1) if flying becomes routine. Never check painted centrepieces if you can avoid it; if forced at the gate, ask for a fragile tag and hand it over at the aircraft door (gate-check) rather than the counter.
- **TSA**: painted plastic/metal miniatures and hobby magnets are permitted in carry-on and checked bags. Expect the bag to be pulled for a hand check (a dense cluster of metal + magnets is opaque on X-ray) — arrive early, keep lids easy to open, and tell the officer they are fragile painted models so they let *you* lift trays out. Not in carry-on: hobby knives/blades, and (practically) sprue cutters; superglue tubes must go in the quart liquids bag; **aerosol primers/varnishes and solvent plastic cement are flammable and are prohibited in both carry-on and checked** — buy at the destination. Dice, tape measures, brushes and acrylic paints (in the liquids bag if carry-on) are fine.
- **Cabin handling**: bag goes under the seat in front (upright) in preference to the overhead; if overhead, load it last, flat side down, and watch other passengers' roller bags.

### 5.2 Quick-start checklist (before you glue anything)
1. Measure the three centrepieces base-to-highest-tip and widest span; write it on masking tape under each base. Buy box height = tallest + 20 mm minimum.
2. Sort every model by base size; flip each and note hollow vs flat underside and any ribs; test-fit one magnet of each size for flushness.
3. Wiggle-test every flight stem, wing, War Dog waist/weapon and staff. Tag anything loose with tape — those get pinned/re-glued tonight *before* magnets.
4. Make the polarity master stack and mark it. Decide "marked face toward steel" and never deviate.
5. Take a magnet to the store when buying trays/sheet; aluminium is useless.
6. Dry-plan the floors on paper (32 L US floor = 15 x 11.9 in; 17 L = 14.9 x 11.5 in): big models >= 3 cm apart and from walls; infantry at ~40 mm pitch in unit blocks (or on movement trays).
