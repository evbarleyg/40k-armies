## THE ONE QUESTION THAT MATTERS MOST — is there a canonical Word Bearers ↔ Be'lakor link?

**Yes — a real but thin one, and it is exactly the right shape for Evan's army.** In *War Zone Charadon Act II: The Book of Fire* (9th ed., July 2021), Be'lakor is brought into realspace in the Charadon war zone by a summoning ritual performed by mortal followers calling themselves his **Disciples** — described in coverage of the book as "the Alpha Legion and Word Bearers, or rather, a group from each of these legions united under the moniker Disciples, [who] completed a summoning ritual and called forth Be'lakor into real space" (http://admechknight.blogspot.com/2021/07/warzone-charadon-book-of-fire-spoilers.html). Lexicanum/Fandom-derived summaries likewise state that Be'lakor, "allying with the Word Bearers Traitor Legion, began his own assault upon the Charadon Sector" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor ; https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor), and that after he stole the Knight World Kolossi and remade it as Korvosi, "Warriors in service to Be'lakor—Traitoris Militarum regiments and Heretic Astartes warbands alike—now congregate around Korvosi" alongside "Word Bearers, the Alpha Legion and other forces including House Korvax" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax ; http://admechknight.blogspot.com/2021/07/warzone-charadon-book-of-fire-spoilers.html). The *Disciples of Be'lakor* Army of Renown in the same book was explicitly built to field Chaos Space Marines and daemons of all four gods together under Be'lakor (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/PJPoYXO8/have-your-cake-and-eat-it-with-belakors-disciples-the-dark-masters-mortal-and-daemonic-legion/), and the current (10th/11th-ed.) **Shadow Legion** detachment is described by GW as "a force of Daemons and Heretic Astartes in thrall to the Dark Master" (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/jl3uxdbu/index-chaos-daemons-updated-datasheets-and-a-new-detachment/).

**What I did NOT find:** no named Word Bearers character, named Host, or Black Library novel/short story tying the XVII Legion to Be'lakor (searches across Horus Heresy, Dawn of Fire, Word Bearers trilogy, White Dwarf returned nothing; the only hit was a fan discussion thread, https://bolterandchainsword.com/topic/300416-30k-word-bearers-belakor/). So: **the link is canonical at the "unnamed Word Bearers groups are among Be'lakor's Disciples" level; everything more specific (which Host, which Dark Apostle, why they turned) is a plausible-framing exercise** — and the canon leaves that space deliberately open, which is ideal for a personal army. Suggested framing is in A3.

# A. LORE

## A1. Be'lakor, the Dark Master

**Identity and titles.** "Be'lakor, the Dark Master, also known as the First Prince of Chaos and the One Who Heralds the Conquerors, is a Daemon Prince of Chaos Undivided; he is beholden to each of the Chaos Gods equally" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor). Fandom's article adds the epithet "the First-Damned" and calls him "the first Daemon Prince ever raised up from mortality by the combined will of the Ruinous Powers" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor). Warhammer Community also uses "the Lord of Torment" for him (https://www.warhammer-community.com/2021/11/17/which-chaos-god-does-belakor-really-serve/). His weapon is the **Blade of Shadows** and his signature defensive rule has been called **Shadow Form** since 9th edition (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/9qhtVMyA/belakors-back-and-the-galaxys-mightiest-warriors-should-be-very-afraid/ ; 10th-ed. datasheet summary at https://wahapedia.ru/wh40k10ed/factions/chaos-daemons/Be-lakor).

**Origin — favoured by all four, then diminished.** "Be'lakor became the first mortal to be blessed by all four Ruinous Powers and became the first Daemon Prince. Granted his dark apotheosis by the combined will of the Chaos Gods as Chaos Undivided, he was given a portion of each of their power" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor). "At first, Be'lakor used the jealousy of the Ruinous Powers to gain their favours, never completely swearing his allegiance to any one of them. For a time, the gods fought over Be'lakor as children might squabble over a favoured toy. However, they soon realised the folly of combining their might into a single vessel, as Be'lakor proved nearly uncontrollable" (same URL). "They soon began to raise up new Daemon Princes, each god choosing only Chaos Champions that would be loyal to them, and them alone. Be'lakor remained the strongest of the Daemon Princes, though his might was diminished as the gods spread their power among their other servants" (same URL; parallel wording at https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor). His mortal species and era are unknown; the lore stresses antiquity: "Legends tell of Be'lakor ruling over mortal empires since the dawn of time, the Daemon Prince conquering a world and subjugating its people, forcing them to worship him as a god. When the race would fall into decline, ruined by Be'lakor's greed and malevolence, the Daemon would move on, finding a new burgeoning race to be his playthings" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor). The 9th-ed. codex review at Mengel Miniatures summarises the same beat: "his origins are shrouded in mystery, and he's been around and worshipped by various cultures for millennia... eternally jealous of playing second fiddle to the chaos gods' favored champions and looking to steal the spotlight for himself" (http://www.mengelminiatures.com/2022/09/review-codex-chaos-daemons.html).

**Character.** "Be'lakor remains a master of shadows, moving behind the veil of history to exert the will of the Chaos Gods upon the universe" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor). "Like a petulant firstborn son, Be'lakor has always had a bitter jealousy toward anything or anyone that wins the favour of the Dark Gods" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor). He is "a being of infinite cunning and absolute cruelty who destroys all that he touches, and jealously covets the favour of the Ruinous Powers, with his only constants being the malice with which he destroys those who oppose him, and his endless jealousy towards those who successfully court the favour of the Gods of Chaos" (AoS Lexicanum wording surfaced in search, https://ageofsigmar.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor).

**The "curse": a pawn who thinks he is a player.** The 40k texts frame his curse less as a single hex and more as perpetual manipulation: "When the Daemon Prince brought down a Champion of Nurgle, invariably he was doing Tzeentch's bidding, and while laying a warrior of the Blood God low he was fulfilling the will of Slaanesh" and "Be'lakor remains blind to these manipulations of the Chaos Gods, with his own thirst for power and the pleasure of proving his mastery over rival Champions of Chaos seeming enough to make him forget the sorcerous tethers the Dark Gods continually try to wind tightly around his neck" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor). In the Warhammer Fantasy/AoS lineage the curse is explicit: he bears "the shame of crowning new champions of Chaos (the current one being Archaon) but never holding that title himself" (Broken Realms review, https://spruesandbrews.com/2021/04/17/broken-realms-belakor-review/). `[uncited — general lore knowledge]` In Warhammer Fantasy it was specifically Tzeentch who cursed him to be the discorporate "Harbinger" who crowns each Everchosen; 40k does not restate that mechanism, but his 40k title "the One Who Heralds the Conquerors" is the echo of it.

**Relationship with each god** (Warhammer Community, "Which Chaos God Does Be'lakor Really Serve?", 17 Nov 2021, https://www.warhammer-community.com/2021/11/17/which-chaos-god-does-belakor-really-serve/ — wording via search summaries):
- *Khorne*: "No matter his shadowy intentions, he finds himself on the battlefield often enough to please Khorne greatly."
- *Tzeentch*: "He's so meddlesome that Tzeentch has chained him up more than a handful of times. Each time the Lord of Torment has escaped."
- *Nurgle*: "Nurgle respects this kind of near-infinite longevity, and Be'lakor's love of cycles – scheme, win, get put in a box by a Chaos god, break out, rinse, and repeat."
- *Slaanesh*: "Everything Be'lakor does, he does it for his insatiable ego."
- *Verdict*: "Ultimately, Be'lakor is too similar to all four gods to serve any of them, which is his biggest strength and weakness. They see themselves in him and vice versa, causing them to lash out in petulant rage at their perceived failings."

**What does the lore say Shadow Legion daemons LOOK like?** Honest answer: **I found no official passage stating that Be'lakor's daemons are physically darkened, monochrome or "shadow-skinned."** `[UNVERIFIED]` What the official language does give you, consistently, is an *environmental* shadow motif you can legitimately paint onto the models:
- Be'lakor himself is "a master of shadows" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor), wields the Blade of Shadows, has the Shadow Form rule, and fights with "shadow-sorcery" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Vashtorr).
- His Disciples are accompanied by a "pall of unnatural shadow" (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/PJPoYXO8/have-your-cake-and-eat-it-with-belakors-disciples-the-dark-masters-mortal-and-daemonic-legion/); their rules are literally "Disciples of Shadow", "Locus of Shadows", the "Noctic" (night) discipline, "Shadow Lord", "Shade Path", "The Shadow's Caress" (URLs above).
- His attack on Kolossi is fought under "Unnatural Darkness" (https://www.goonhammer.com/war-zone-charadon-the-book-of-fire-review-the-narrative-play-rules/), and the world he keeps is "a Fallen Knight World of mist and shadow, reshaped by the hand of the Dark Master... now enshrouded by a darkness so vast that it stretches into the surrounding void of space", with "mist-wreathed keeps on the penumbral Fallen Knight World of Korvosi" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax); GW's own House Korvax painting video calls the house "mist-shrouded" (https://www.facebook.com/WarhammerOfficial/videos/how-to-paint-house-korvax-chaos-knights/363293892433030/).
- In AoS the sky itself darkens into the Cursed Skies with "leering, daemonic faces" in the clouds (https://ageofsigmar.lexicanum.com/wiki/Cursed_Skies) and he employs literal "shadow daemons" of Ulgu (https://ageofsigmar.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor).
So the defensible narrative claim for the vision document is: *the Legion marches inside Be'lakor's shadow* — a travelling penumbra, mist and unnatural night — rather than "the daemons' skin turned grey." The monochrome/desaturated look is a (very popular) hobby interpretation of that motif; see B6–B7.

## A2. Why daemons of all four gods serve together under him, and how mortals fit

**In-lore justifications you can cite.**
1. *He carries a portion of each god's power*, granted at his apotheosis "by the combined will of the Chaos Gods as Chaos Undivided" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor) — he is the one being with standing in all four courts, "beholden to each of the Chaos Gods equally" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor).
2. *Mixed motives inside the Legion*: GW's own descriptor — "a vast host of Daemons – some coerced, some enslaved, and some that find common cause in the destruction of empires" (https://www.goonhammer.com/45821-2, quoting the Broken Realms preview). That single sentence licenses everything from press-ganged Bloodletters to Tzeentchian opportunists.
3. *The gods permit it because it serves the Great Game*: whenever he wins, one god or another profits ("bringing down a Champion of Nurgle, invariably he was doing Tzeentch's bidding..."), and they keep "sorcerous tethers" on him (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor). A rival-god contingent in his Legion can be read as that god's stake in (and leash on) the enterprise.
4. *He is a serial subverter of other powers' servants*: House Raven's garrison turned "in Be'lakor's name" after he "whispered into the minds of Kolossi's Sacristans" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax); Eternus is a stolen Stormcast (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/5R01u7Cr/black-lightning-strikes-as-eternus-blade-of-the-first-prince-storms-into-battle/); in AoS he runs "a legion of spies and agents – mortals, daemons, and even stranger beings" (https://ageofsigmar.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor); across the Black Crusades he manipulated the Daemon Prince Tallomin and insinuated himself into Abaddon's inner circle (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor).
5. *The 2025+ rules text bakes the concept in*: the Shadow Legion detachment rule "grant[s] a unique benefit according to which God each Daemon unit belongs to, or whether it is a mortal follower" (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/jl3uxdbu/index-chaos-daemons-updated-datasheets-and-a-new-detachment/) — i.e., GW's current model of the Legion is precisely "four gods' daemons + mortal thralls, each keeping its own nature, unified by service to Be'lakor."

**Mortal followers.** The umbrella term in 40k is **Disciples of Be'lakor**: "the forces of Chaos — including Daemons drawn from the legions of all the Chaos Gods, Chaos Space Marines, Traitoris Militarum regiments and the Chaos Knights of House Korvax — under the command of the first Daemon Prince... also known as the Shadow Legion" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Disciples_of_Be'lakor). Canonical mortal components named in coverage: Word Bearers and Alpha Legion splinters "united under the moniker Disciples" (http://admechknight.blogspot.com/2021/07/warzone-charadon-book-of-fire-spoilers.html); "Traitoris Militarum regiments and Heretic Astartes warbands" gathering at Korvosi (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax); House Korvax itself (A4). GW's current phrase for the mortals is Heretic Astartes "in thrall to the Dark Master" / "mortal follower[s]" (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/jl3uxdbu/index-chaos-daemons-updated-datasheets-and-a-new-detachment/).
- `[UNVERIFIED]` **"Shadow-marked."** I could not confirm that GW uses "shadow-marked" (or a specific brand/rune of Be'lakor) for his mortal followers, nor find named Disciples warbands/regiments/characters from the Book of Fire. Treat "shadow-marked" as your own coinage unless the helper session turns up a source. What *is* sourced is that his mortals fight under a "pall of unnatural shadow" and swap their Legion identity for "Disciples of Shadow" (URLs above) — which is a neat in-rules metaphor for a Word Bearers Host that has set aside Lorgar's Word.

## A3. Word Bearers and daemonkind; the Word Bearers ↔ Be'lakor question

**The Legion and daemons — sourced points.**
- The Word Bearers are the XVII Legion, "pledged to their Daemon Primarch Lorgar and to Chaos Undivided" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Word_Bearers). Erebus, "harbinger of the Horus Heresy, instrumental in converting first Lorgar and then Horus to Chaos", was First Chaplain and had "whole sections of the Book of Lorgar tattooed across his shaven scalp"; he sits on "the Dark Council of Sicarus" (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Erebus ; https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Erebus).

The Priestess Ingethel of Cadia would initiate a ritual that saw her transformed into the Daemon Prince known as Ingethel the Ascended, and then led the 1,301st Expeditionary Fleet's scout vessel Orfeo's Lament into the Eye of Terror" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Pilgrimage_of_Lorgar ; https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Gal_Vorbak). "In battle they could shed their veil of seeming and become true monsters, fusions of armour, bone and muscle charged with supernatural power. Their flesh and Ceramite fused and warped into a new, bestial form, marking them out as amongst the first of the Possessed Chaos Space Marines" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Gal_Vorbak). **Word Bearers ↔ Be'lakor: verdict and framing.** As set out at the top: canon gives you *unnamed Word Bearers groups among the Disciples who summoned Be'lakor at Charadon and now muster at Korvosi* (http://admechknight.blogspot.com/2021/07/warzone-charadon-book-of-fire-spoilers.html ; https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax ; https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Be'lakor), and current rules text for "Heretic Astartes in thrall to the Dark Master" (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/jl3uxdbu/index-chaos-daemons-updated-datasheets-and-a-new-detachment/). No named Host/character/novel link was found. The most lore-plausible framings, in descending order of canon support:
1. **"A Host among the Disciples" (canon-adjacent).** Evan's Word Bearers are one of the XVII Legion splinters that "united under the moniker Disciples" to perform the Charadon summoning, and have followed the Dark Master since, mustering out of mist-shrouded Korvosi in Imperium Nihilus. This needs no invention beyond a Host name and a Dark Apostle/Coryphaus. It also explains the two War Dogs (Korvax-adjacent Dreadblades, A4).
2. **"Turned from the Word to the First Prince" (thematic).** Word Bearers theology already venerates Chaos Undivided and treats daemons as divine; Be'lakor is the *first and purest* product of Undivided apotheosis ("granted... by the combined will of the Chaos Gods as Chaos Undivided", https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Be%27lakor). A Dark Apostle concluding that the First Prince, not the absent Lorgar or the squabbling Dark Council, is the true "Word made flesh" is heresy-within-heresy that fits the Legion's schismatic history (34th Host power struggles, Erebus/Kor Phaeron factionalism — https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Coryphaus ; https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Erebus). Mechanically this is even mirrored by the rules: Disciples CSM *replace their Legion trait* with Disciples of Shadow (https://www.goonhammer.com/war-zone-charadon-the-book-of-fire-review-chaos/).
3. **"Subverted, like House Raven" (Be'lakor's M.O.).** Be'lakor "whispered into the minds" of Kolossi's Sacristans until a third of a loyal house turned "in Be'lakor's name" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax) and stole a Stormcast champion outright (Eternus, https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/5R01u7Cr/black-lightning-strikes-as-eternus-blade-of-the-first-prince-storms-into-battle/). A Host whose Master of Possession opened one door too many and found the Dark Master waiting is entirely in character — "thrall-host" is then literal.
Recommendation: combine 1 + 2 (they were at Charadon by choice, and the choice has become a creed), keep 3 as the whispered doubt inside the Host.

- **God identity on a dark body — the transferable principle.** Even without the paint lists, the official material above converges on one grammar: body = Be'lakor's palette (black → cold grey, sometimes with a bruise-purple or ember under-glow); identity = *light* (blade-flame, eyes, fire). **Recurring techniques** (from the verified items plus `[uncited — general hobby knowledge]` of how these armies are usually done — present to Evan as options, not as a census):
- *Monochrome body + god-colour light*: black/charcoal skin drybrushed or stippled up through cold greys; the god's colour confined to emissive bits (eyes, blades, flames, sores, tongues), often with a little OSL bleed onto adjacent skin. Cheapest way to make four gods read as one legion; matches the 'Eavy Metal Be'lakor grammar.
- *"Shadow gradient"*: model painted in normal god colours but extremities/lower body glazed progressively to black (or the reverse — black body, colour only at the extremities), selling "emerging from Be'lakor's shadow."
- *Whole-army OSL from the base* (Max Soule): identity kept in full colour, unity supplied by one light source and one basing recipe.
- *Classic god colours + ruthless shared basing/rims + one shared accent* (e.g., the same brass, the same purple-black smoke, the same pale spectral green as Be'lakor's sword on every unit's magical effects).
- *Basing as the shadow*: dark ash/slate bases with drifting mist (cotton/water-effects) or painted creeping shadow tendrils, echoing Korvosi's "mist and shadow" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/House_Korvax).

## B8. Word Bearers visual canon

**Official scheme, in words.** Heresy-to-40k Word Bearers wear deep crimson ("Traitor's Red", first worn by the Gal Vorbak, replacing Legion grey — https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Gal_Vorbak) with metallic trim, and armour "decorate[d]... in all manner of additional religious markings, including Colchisian runes etched into the plating and lines of blasphemous scripture" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Word_Bearers). GW's official video tutorial is *How to Paint: Horus Heresy Word Bearers* (Warhammer YouTube, 2022, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9RmulwgOy30) and the current official written recipes live in the *Traitor Legiones Astartes Painting Guide* PDF (2025, https://assets.warhammer-community.com/the_horus_heresy_traitor_legiones_astartes_painting_guide_2025-olx7t82j3w-vsf1kmgbb8.pdf) `[contents not opened — UNVERIFIED which paints it lists]`. `[uncited — general hobby knowledge]` Citadel sells two paints literally named for the Legion — **Gal Vorbak Red** (Base) and **Word Bearers Red** (Layer) — and the long-standing Citadel-system route for the brighter 40k crimson is Khorne Red based, shaded dark, then highlighted up through Wazdakka Red / Squig Orange.

**Concrete recipes found (community, paint-by-paint):**
- *Classic Citadel crimson*: "Wash with Carroburg Crimson, layer with Khorne Red, then Wazdakka Red, and finish with Squig Orange as an extreme highlight. For silver trimming, basecoat with Leadbelcher, wash with Nuln Oil, then layer with Ironbreaker" (http://blogsofwarbk.blogspot.com/2012/10/how-i-paint-word-bearers.html). Spray variant: "Spray Khorne Red... then Wazdakka Red from a higher angle, followed by applying Carroburg Crimson wash directly to recessed areas. A wash of Nuln Oil is applied onto all silver areas and recesses" (https://primarchs.wordpress.com/2013/03/26/painting-word-bearers/).
- *'Eavy-Metal-style edge highlighting on dark red*: "a chunky edge highlight of Khorne Red and Squig Orange mixed 1:1, followed by a fine line of Squig Orange, then a 1:1 mix of Squig Orange and Lugganath Orange for selective highlights on pronounced edges and corners"; trim shading "two coats of Nuln Oil and Lahmian Medium mixed 1:1" (https://paintpad.app/recipes/1424-word-bearers-armour-2021 ; https://paintpad.app/recipes/1694-word-bearers).
- *Darker Heresy maroon using the named paints*: "(1) Gal Vorbak Red basecoat, (2) second layer of Gal Vorbak Red, (3) lining recesses with Leviathan Purple, (4) layer of Word Bearers Red, (5) initial highlights of Word Bearers Red/Fire Dragon Bright mix, (6) secondary highlights of Fire Dragon Bright, (7) Winsor & Newton red ink glaze" (https://heresyhub.fandom.com/wiki/Word_Bearers); alternative "black primer, base Barak-Nar Burgundy, then glazing with Khorne Red and Wazdakka Red covering less and less of the armour" (https://paintpad.app/recipes/1697-word-bearers-for-horus-heresy).
- *Fast army method (RichyP, Goonhammer HTPE: Word Bearers)*: "Prime everything with Mephiston Red... wash the armor in Citadel Wyldwood Contrast and drybrush the entire model in Vallejo Game Color Scarlet Blood. Paint the shoulder pads and black areas in Black Templar Contrast... Black Legion also works, but the thinness of the Black Templar will give you a bit more variation in tone." The same article "covers purity seals and Colchisian runes, heresy and modern marines, Gal Vorbak and possessed, and Lorgar" (https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/ ; mirror https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/ ; heraldry example image https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/wordbearers_heraldry_example/).
- Further tutorials: FauxHammer "How to paint Word Bearers Armour" (2019, https://www.fauxhammer.com/tutorials/how-to-paint-word-bearers-armour-tutorial-2019/), Corehammer 30k traitor Word Bearers (http://www.corehammer.com/how-to-paint-horus-heresy-30k-traitor-word-bearers/), The Beard Bunker "Bearers of the Word" (https://www.thebeardbunker.com/2020/05/bearers-of-word.html), Apologist's "inload: Bearers of the Word" (https://apologentsia.blogspot.com/2019/10/inload-bearers-of-word.html), Brush & Boltgun video (https://brushandboltgun.com/2022/05/11/video-how-to-paint-word-bearers/), Plaguenarr scheme notes (https://www.plaguenarr.com/word-bearers-color-scheme/), and a Gal Vorbak/Argel Tal video tutorial (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWqa0NI-CrI).
- **Note for Evan's red/black models:** RichyP's Goonhammer recipe (red armour, Black Templar shoulder pads/black areas) shows red-and-black is an established Word Bearers presentation, not a deviation (https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/).

**Heraldry & script placement.**
- Legion icon: the **Latros Sacrum**, "a roaring, horned daemon's head wreathed in flames" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Word_Bearers); the pre-Heresy burning-book device is the alternative (icon discussion: https://bolterandchainsword.com/topic/318234-word-bearers-legion-icon-question/). Chapter/Host badges are simple emblem-glyphs (morning star for Graven Star, portcullis for Exalted Gate, flame-crowned skull for Tri-fold Crown — https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Word_Bearers).
- Script: cuneiform lines on flat armour panels, trims and greaves and on vehicles' red honour panels (https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Colchisian_Torquatii ; https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Word_Bearers); hobbyists generate it from Old Persian cuneiform (https://bolterandchainsword.com/topic/262497-writing-in-colchisian-style/). `[uncited — general hobby knowledge]` Typical execution is bone/white or gold micro-lettering on the red, black lettering on parchment strips, and facial/scalp script tattoos on bare heads (after Erebus, whose scalp bears the Book of Lorgar — https://wh40k.lexicanum.com/wiki/Erebus).
- **Transfer sheets.** `[uncited — general hobby knowledge; VERIFY against current product pages]` The Horus Heresy *Word Bearers Legion Transfer Sheet* carries Legion icons in several sizes (flaming daemon head and burning book variants), blocks and lines of Colchisian cuneiform, vehicle-scale icons and numerals; the 40k Chaos Space Marines transfer sheet includes Word Bearers Legion icons among the Legion badges. I could not open a product page this session to itemise them.

**Possessed / Gal Vorbak as the marine↔daemon bridge.** Canon basis: Gal Vorbak "flesh and Ceramite fused and warped into a new, bestial form" (https://warhammer40k.fandom.com/wiki/Gal_Vorbak). Hobby handling found: Goonhammer's HTPE Word Bearers has a dedicated Gal Vorbak/Possessed section (https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/); Tale of Painters' tutorial for **purple Possessed flesh** (https://taleofpainters.com/2023/04/tutorial-how-to-paint-purple-chaos-possessed-flesh/) is a ready-made way to make Possessed flesh rhyme with a cold/purple-shadow daemon palette rather than with warm human skin. `[uncited — general hobby knowledge]` The three standard bridges are (a) daemon flesh erupting from red plate painted in the *daemon contingent's* skin colour, (b) armour that grows horn/bone in the trim colour, and (c) inner glow (embers/warpfire) in the cracks matching whatever emissive colour the daemons use — for a Shadow Legion that would be Be'lakor's pale spectral blade-light or the Tzeentch warpflame already on Evan's Flamers/Horrors.

**Word Bearers explicitly themed to Be'lakor / painted darker.** `[UNVERIFIED]` none found this session beyond the canonical Book of Fire association; the sourced fact that Disciples CSM keep their own Legion *models* but swap their Legion *trait* (https://www.goonhammer.com/war-zone-charadon-the-book-of-fire-review-chaos/) and that House Korvax keeps a conventional dark livery plus "mist" (B6) both argue for: keep the Word Bearers recognisably Word Bearers (crimson, script, Latros Sacrum), and signal thralldom with *additions* — a Be'lakor device or shadow-glyph where a Host badge would sit, blackened lower armour/greaves fading up into crimson, cold spectral accent colour shared with the daemons, and the army-wide shadow/mist basing.
