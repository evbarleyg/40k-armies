# Making a Second-Hand "Shadow Legion" Read as One Army
### A cohesion playbook for Evan: pre-painted Be'lakor daemons + Word Bearers + War Dogs, with room to grow

**Prepared:** 2026-08-11. **Audience:** first-army hobbyist with brushes, rattle-can primer, no airbrush assumed (airbrush-optional paths are given everywhere).

**How to read the labels**
- **[B] Beginner-safe** = forgiving, reversible or low-stakes; **[A] Advanced** = needs brush control/patience or is hard to undo.
- **Citations:** every recipe/claim carries either a source URL, or the tag **[uncited — general hobby knowledge]** where it comes from long-established Citadel-system practice that could not be re-verified in this session (see "Research constraints" at the end). Paint names are Citadel unless stated; common equivalents are in the appendix.
- Times are hands-on brush time for a careful beginner and exclude drying (texture pastes and washes need 30 min to overnight).

---

## 0. TL;DR — "Minimum Viable Cohesion" (the 20% that buys 80%)

Do these five things, in this order, before any repainting. Each is [B].

1. **One basing scheme + one rim colour on every model** (Section 5, Scheme A "pale ash" recommended). Consistent basing is the single strongest unifier of models painted by different hands — it ties a force together "even if different units have slightly different colour schemes" (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-base-warhammer-models/). ~12–15 min/infantry base, done over the existing base without removing the model.
2. **One matte varnish over everything.** Second-hand lots mix satin, gloss and chalky finishes; a single ultra-matte coat makes them read as one paint job and protects them (https://taleofpainters.com/2023/02/video-review-the-5-best-matt-varnishes-for-warhammer-miniatures-spray-airbrush-ultra-matt/ ; https://ageofminiatures.com/best-varnish-for-miniatures/).
3. **"Shadow creep" on every daemon:** 2–4 thinned dark glazes on the lower third of each daemon so feet/legs sink to near-black and the god colour survives up top (Section 2.2). ~5–8 min per infantry model.
4. **One shared light:** the same pale, cold "Blade of Shadows" ghost-glow (white core, pale blue halo) in every daemon's eyes and on one focal item per unit, plus the same glow leaking from base cracks/runes. A single saturated spot colour repeated army-wide is a classic unifier (https://creativetwilight.com/osl/ ; http://www.mengelminiatures.com/2019/11/mixing-your-own-colors.html).
5. **One trim metal across Word Bearers, War Dogs and Be'lakor's armour** (Section 3.2/4): pick warm bronze/brass and use the identical 3-paint recipe on all of them.

Everything else in this document is optional polish layered on top of those five.

## 1. Unifying a pre-painted, mixed army — the general playbook

### 1(a) Rebasing everything onto one scheme

**Rule one: do not prise painted plastic models off their bases unless the base size is wrong.** Re-surface the existing base instead: scrape loose flock/sand with an old blade or stiff toothbrush, then apply texture paste right over what remains and around the feet. Texture paints are "the fastest, easiest way" — apply with an old brush or sculpting tool, let dry, wash, drybrush, tuft, paint the rim; roughly 5 minutes of brush time per model (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-base-warhammer-models/ ; https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-paint-bases-quickly/). Write the recipe down (product names, rim colour, tuft SKU) so additions in a year still match (same source).

**When you must remove a model from its base** (wrong base size, resin scenic base you dislike, metal model on a broken slotta):
- Superglue joints go brittle when frozen — a spell in the freezer lets metal (and often plastic-on-superglue) figures pop off; plastic-cement (welded) joints will not release and must be cut (http://theminiaturespage.com/boards/msg.mv?id=472809 ; https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/656480.page).
- Otherwise chip away basing grit near the feet with a spike, slide a fresh scalpel/Stanley blade flat under the foot, and lever gently; or sacrifice the base — clip the rim away with sprue cutters and sand the remaining disc thin, then glue that disc onto the new base and bury it in texture paste (http://csl.stanford.edu/~pal/peter/rebasing/ ; https://dedard.blogspot.com/2015/02/how-to-rebase-miniatures.html).
- Do not soak based models in Pine-Sol-type strippers to free them — plastic bases (and models) go soft (http://www.how-to-paint-miniatures.com/miniature-painting-removing-paint.html).
- **Base-size adapters:** if older daemons arrive on 25 mm rounds, glue the old base inside a 25-to-32 mm adapter ring (3D-printed/third-party "base adapter rings") and texture over the seam — zero risk to the paint. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- What goes wrong: snapped ankles (Bloodletters and Horrors have thin legs — always cut the base, never twist the model); knife slips (cut away from fingers, fresh blade); frost condensation on frozen models (let them return to room temperature before painting). [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Texture pastes (pick one family and stick to it):** Citadel Texture (Astrogranite / Astrogranite Debris for grey ash-rubble; Stirland Mud / Armageddon Dust for earth; Mordant Earth crackle for obsidian/lava), Vallejo Earth Texture / AK Terrains tubs (far cheaper per ml for 70 bases), or PVA + sand + paint. Astrogranite family review and how-to: https://tangibleday.com/citadel-technical-paint-astrogranite-review/.

**A scheme that suits shadow daemons + crimson marines:** you want the base to be *lighter and more neutral* than the models so near-black daemons silhouette against it, with one accent that echoes the army glow. Hence the recommendation of **pale ash / scorched temple dust** (Section 5, Scheme A), optionally with obsidian crackle patches or flagstone fragments. Dark bases under dark models make the army vanish at arm's length. [uncited — general hobby knowledge; the value-contrast principle is discussed in https://birdwithabrush.com/2022/03/06/how-to-paint-bases-like-a-boss/]

**Rim colour conventions:** the long-time GW studio default is a mid-brown (Steel Legion Drab); black rims (Abaddon Black) are the modern default for display and for dark/urban/ash schemes; grey rims (Mechanicus Standard Grey) are used with Astrogranite urban bases (https://hobbithobbies.com/2017/08/23/quick-easy-cohesive-bases/ uses Astrogranite Debris + Mechanicus Standard Grey rim; general rim discussion https://birdwithabrush.com/2022/03/06/how-to-paint-bases-like-a-boss/). **Recommendation: Abaddon Black rims, two thin coats, on everything** — it frames pale ash cleanly and matches the shadow theme. Whatever you choose, never mix rim colours within the army. [recommendation = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 1(b) Unifying filters and glazes over existing paint

**What a glaze is and how far it can pull schemes together.** A glaze is a near-transparent tint: paint thinned roughly 1:4–1:6 with Lahmian Medium / Vallejo Glaze Medium (medium keeps the binder intact so it does not go chalky or bead), or about 1 drop paint : 2+ drops water, applied in a thin, controlled film — "just retaining a whisper of the original colour" (https://warhammerguild.com/glazing-tutorial/ ; https://thearmypainter.com/blogs/explore/what-is-glazing-miniature-painting ; https://tangibleday.com/how-to-glaze-miniatures-tutorial-for-blending-color/). Washes/glazes "help unify the colour scheme … by tying together disparate colours" (https://painting-miniatures.com/how-to-glaze-miniatures/). Vince Venturella's Hobby Cheating episodes on glazes/filters are the standard video primers: HC 122 How to Glaze https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N88NtHNmz1Q ; HC 255 Thinning, Glazes & Filters https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TbCtUYFwFWQ ; HC 10 Washes, Glazes & Drybrush https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qaARYpmCyC4.

Realistic expectations [uncited — general hobby knowledge]:
- A unifying glaze can shift *temperature and value* (make five different reds all read "dark crimson"; make bright daemons read "shadowed"), and can kill garish highlights. Two to four coats can move a colour roughly one "step" on the Citadel ladder (Evil Sunz Scarlet toward Khorne Red).
- It cannot change hue family (blue armour will not become red), fix sloppy edges, or hide texture from thick paint. If the underlying job is lumpy, glazing makes it glossy-lumpy.
- Glazes darken. Plan for the army to end up darker than any of its current parts — which is exactly what Shadow Legion wants.

**Three unifying-filter options, least to most committal:**

1. **[B] Acrylic shade/Contrast glaze (recommended).** Mix Contrast or Shade 1:2–1:4 with Contrast Medium/Lahmian Medium; apply top-down with a size 2 brush, wicking pools off feet and undercuts with a damp clean brush before they dry. For daemons: Black Templar or Basilicanum Grey + medium; for reds: Berserker Bloodshade / Carroburg Crimson / Flesh Tearers Red + medium (Section 3.5). Dark Contrast paints strongly mute what is beneath, so go thinner than you think and build coats (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/contrast-over-zenithal-guide/). What goes wrong: tide marks (too much liquid — unload the brush; work one limb at a time), reactivating earlier Contrast layers on second-hand models painted with Contrast (if colour lifts, stop, matte-varnish, then glaze over the varnish). [reactivation caveat = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
2. **[B+/A] Oil or enamel filter.** A dot of black/sepia artist oil thinned heavily with odourless white spirit, or AK/Ammo enamel filters and Streaking Grime, flowed over the model then, after ~10 minutes, feathered/removed with a spirit-damp flat brush. Enamels can go straight over touch-dry acrylics without harming them; a satin/gloss coat first buys working time and easier clean-up (https://www.rebelscale.com/techniques/enamel-weathering/ ; https://aztoyhobby.com/blogs/news/ak-interactive-streaking-grime-explained ; beginner Streaking Grime walk-through https://discourse.chaos-dwarfs.com/t/streaking-grime-speedpaint-a-beginners-guide/24902). Superb for the War Dogs and for grimy Word Bearers; less suited to smooth daemon skin. What goes wrong: white spirit can craze bare polystyrene if it floods joints, and enamel left thick stays tacky for days — thin coats, ventilate, 24 h before varnish. [caveats = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
3. **[B] Matte varnish as the "sheen filter".** Even with zero colour work, one flat coat removes the gloss/satin/chalk patchwork that screams "three previous owners". AK Ultra Matte (brush/airbrush) is the flattest brush-on but slightly fragile, so put a satin/gloss coat under it on gaming pieces; Testors Dullcote is the benchmark rattle-can (lacquer — ventilate); Vallejo Mecha matt is a tough water-based option; spray only below ~60% humidity at 15–25 °C from 25–30 cm in light passes to avoid frosting; water-based brush-on varnishes essentially cannot frost (https://ageofminiatures.com/best-varnish-for-miniatures/ ; https://tangibleday.com/best-matte-varnish-for-miniatures-testors-dullcote-review/ ; https://taleofpainters.com/2022/02/top-tip-stahlys-best-matt-varnishes-for-painting-miniatures-spray-brush-on/ ; https://thearmypainter.com/blogs/explore/how-to-use-a-varnish ; Goonhammer/Tabletop Battles Hobby 101 varnish https://www.tabletopbattles.com/hobby-101-how-to-use-varnish-with-miniatures/). Re-gloss gems, eyes, slime and blood afterwards with 'Ardcoat. [re-gloss tip = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 1(c) Shared spot colours and a shared light source

- **One accent colour mixed into everything.** Mixing the same highlight colour into the top highlight of every material "helps tie them all together" (http://www.mengelminiatures.com/2019/11/mixing-your-own-colors.html). For this army: a pale cold off-white (Ulthuan Grey / Blue Horror) as the final dot highlight on daemon skin, WB armour lenses, and War Dog lights.
- **One light.** OSL reads best when the glow colour is very saturated, is the brightest value on the model, and falls off in straight lines from the source (https://creativetwilight.com/osl/ ; https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-object-source-lighting-osl/ ; https://thearmypainter.com/blogs/explore/how-to-do-object-source-lighting). Pick ONE army light and repeat it: recommended **"ghost-light"** = white core → Blue Horror/Baharroth Blue halo → Aethermatic Blue-tinted surroundings (the 20-minute drybrush-then-Contrast method, Section 2.3). God colours stay on weapons/flames as *secondary* accents. Alternative if you choose lava/candle bases: make the army light warm (Flash Gitz Yellow → Fire Dragon Bright → Khorne Red) and keep the cold glow only on Be'lakor's blade. Do not run both at equal strength — one must dominate. [recommendation = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 1(d) Touch-up triage for eBay minis

1. **Inspect and sort** on arrival into four trays: Keep / Glaze / Partial repaint / Strip (rules in 1(e)). Photograph units so you can colour-match later. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
2. **Clean:** lukewarm water + a drop of dish soap + soft toothbrush removes finger grease and dust that stop glazes and varnish wetting evenly; rinse, air-dry overnight. Never hot water on resin (warps). [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
3. **Repair:** re-glue breaks with plastic cement (plastic-to-plastic) or superglue + a pin for heavy resin/metal parts; fill gaps with Vallejo Plastic Putty or green stuff. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
4. **Chips:** spot-prime bare plastic with brush-on primer (Vallejo Surface Primer black/grey) or simply two thin coats of a Base paint, then feather the local colour back; a unifying glaze afterwards hides the join. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
5. **Thick or clogged paint:** if detail (teeth, runes, trim rivets) is filled, no glaze will save it — strip that model (below). If paint is merely a bit heavy but detail reads, keep it: matte varnish, glaze, move on. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
6. **Re-priming over existing paint:** only over sound, thin, clean, fully matte paint; dust light passes rather than a wet coat; never spray over gloss varnish or greasy models (poor adhesion) — scuff with matte varnish first or strip. Brush-on primer is safer for partial areas (e.g., re-doing only a War Dog's trim). [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Stripping — only when necessary, and by material:**
- **Isopropyl alcohol 91–99% (plastic, metal; resin with care):** 30–40 min softens most acrylics; up to 24 h for stubborn coats; scrub with a toothbrush; leaves no greasy residue. Do not leave resin beyond ~24 h and check it periodically (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-strip-paint-miniatures/ ; https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/697164.page ; https://www.fauxhammer.com/tutorials/strip-paint-off-miniatures-cheap-and-easy/).
- **LA's Totally Awesome (US dollar-store degreaser):** use neat in a lidded tub, soak several hours/overnight, scrub; budget alternative to Simple Green; test resin in short cycles (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-strip-paint-miniatures/ ; https://tangibleday.com/10-best-paint-strippers-miniatures-plastic-resin-metal/).
- **Biostrip 20 (UK water-based gel):** 4–24 h, no fumes, brush it on, scrub off; fine on GW plastic and metal, but reports of resin going soft and of some recent GW plastics softening/crumbling on long soaks — keep soaks short and test (https://www.fauxhammer.com/tutorials/strip-paint-off-miniatures-cheap-and-easy/ ; https://forum.oldhammer.org/threads/paint-stripping.40268/ ; https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-strip-paint-miniatures/).
- **Dettol (brown antiseptic, UK/AUS):** 20-minute cycles then scrub; goes grey and gloopy after a couple of dozen models — replace it; **never let water touch Dettol-softened paint** (it turns into sticky white goo) — clean up with neat washing-up liquid first, water last (http://realmofchaos80s.blogspot.com/2014/01/how-to-use-dettol-to-best-clean-up.html ; https://www.tga.community/forums/topic/3513-dettol-stripping-problem/ ; https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/322635.page ; https://forum.oldhammer.org/threads/stripping-paint-from-old-minis.17358/).
- **Metal** tolerates anything, including acetone, indefinitely; **never acetone on plastic** (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-strip-paint-miniatures/).
- General second-hand rescue guides: https://spikeybits.com/tips-on-buying-used-armies-ebay-miniature-rescue/ ; https://handfulofdice.com/2022/04/22/step-by-step-guide-stripping-warhammer-miniatures/ ; https://warhamateur.com/tools/paints/stripping-warhammer-models-the-easy-way/.
- What goes wrong: superglued assemblies fall apart in IPA (bag parts per model); primer rarely comes off 100% — that is fine, prime over it; gloves for everything, ventilation for IPA. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 1(e) Decision rules: keep / glaze / partial repaint / strip

| Test (in this order) | Verdict | Typical time |
|---|---|---|
| Detail clogged, paint cracked/gloopy, or gloss enamel of unknown type | **Strip** and repaint via the "new arrivals" recipes | 30 min soak + 15 min scrub + full repaint |
| Sound paint, but hue family is wrong for the plan (e.g., bright blue Word Bearers, yellow daemons) | **Partial repaint**: re-base-coat the offending areas only (armour panels; daemon skin via slapchop over a black re-prime of the skin), keep metals/details | 30–60 min per infantry model |
| Right hue family, wrong value/saturation/finish (too bright, too glossy, mismatched reds, neon daemons) | **Glaze** (unifying filter + shadow creep) + rebase + varnish | 8–15 min per infantry model |
| Right hues, tidy, just "not mine" | **Keep**: rebase, shared glow dots, varnish | 5–8 min + base |

Bias hard toward Keep/Glaze: on a 70-model lot, stripping more than the worst ~10% is where beginner projects stall. [table = uncited — general hobby knowledge, consistent with the rescue guides above]

## 2. The "Shadow Legion" daemon look

### 2.1 The visual language (what we are aiming at)
Be'lakor's own studio/box-art scheme is near-black skin worked up through cold greys, dark bronze-gold armour, torn dark membranes with bruised purple-red in the tears, and a pale, cold flame on the Blade of Shadows (Goonhammer HTPE Be'lakor: https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everthing-belakor/ = https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everthing-belakor/ ; Paint Pad box-art-style recipe: https://paintpad.app/recipes/3837-belakor-the-dark-master). Warhammer Community's featured alternative Be'lakor built the skin from "thousands of individual dots of varying tones" — stippling that reads textured but stays smooth (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/p8aiOYqn/this-bright-and-beautiful-belakor-paint-job-is-chefs-kiss/). The Shadow Legion detachment itself pairs Be'lakor's daemons with Chaos Space Marines (https://www.tabletopbattles.com/detachment-focus-shadow-legion/ ; https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/PJPoYXO8/have-your-cake-and-eat-it-with-belakors-disciples-the-dark-masters-mortal-and-daemonic-legion/).

So the army rule is: **value does the unifying, hue does the identifying.** Every daemon is 60–80% near-black/dark desaturated; its god colour lives only in (i) the upper body fading in from black legs ("shadow creep"), (ii) recess-glow or extremities (blades, tongues, flames, sores, eyes), and (iii) one shared cold ghost-light. [synthesis = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 2.2 Four ways to make "shadow creep" (black feet fading up into colour)

1. **[B] Glaze-down (best for RETROFITTING painted models).** Mix Black Templar (cool) or Basilicanum Grey (neutral) Contrast 1:1 with Contrast Medium. Coat 1: from the soles up to mid-thigh. Dry. Coat 2: soles to knee. Coat 3: soles to ankle. Finish feet/hooves with near-opaque Corvus Black. Feather each top edge upward with a damp clean brush while wet. On pink/blue Tzeentch use purple/indigo instead of black for the upper coats (2.5) so it does not go muddy. Principle of stacked, receding glaze bands: https://warhammerguild.com/glazing-tutorial/ ; behaviour of dark Contrast over existing colour: https://warhammerguild.com/painting/contrast-over-zenithal-guide/. Black Templar vs Black Legion behave differently (Templar is a translucent blue-black that shades; Black Legion is a near-opaque flat black — use it only for the final foot band): https://mixedsignals.ml/games/blog/blog_black-templar-and-black-legion. 5–8 min per infantry model, 20–30 min per monster.
2. **[B] Sponge/stipple soot.** Torn blister foam or a cheap stippling brush, Abaddon Black/Corvus Black, most of it dabbed off on paper; stipple densely at the feet, sparser up the shins, a few specks at mid-thigh. Gives a smoky "dissolving into shadow" texture that matches the stippled-skin Be'lakor above (https://www.warhammer-community.com/en-gb/articles/p8aiOYqn/this-bright-and-beautiful-belakor-paint-job-is-chefs-kiss/). Combine with method 1 (glaze first, stipple the transition). [application specifics = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
3. **[B] Drybrush-up (best for NEW models).** Prime black. Heavy drybrush of the darkest god colour from the waist up only; medium drybrush of the mid tone chest-up; light drybrush of the highlight on head/shoulders/forearms/weapon hand. Legs stay black with a faint grey drybrush for definition. This is slapchop with the greyscale confined to the top of the model (https://tangibleday.com/zenithal-dry-brushing-and-slapchop-painting-miniatures/ ; Hobby 101 Drybrushing https://www.tabletopbattles.com/hobby-101-drybrushing). Drybrushing tips — nearly dry bristles, light touch, build slowly: https://tangibleday.com/citadel-technical-paint-astrogranite-review/.
4. **[A / airbrush-optional] Spray gradient.** Black primer; zenithal grey/white from directly above only; Contrast/ink god colour through the airbrush or by brush; then re-darken from below with thinned black ink sprayed upward at 45° ("nadir shading"). Vince Venturella HC 75 Zenithal + Glazes https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-dQU-dSNa8 ; HC 02 Undershading & Glazes https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4ZFXSE9UHUM ; airbrushed Be'lakor skin (Next Level Painting) https://spikeybits.com/warhammer-40k/how-to-airbrush-belakors-skin-next-level-painting-tutorial/. Rattle-can substitute: black primer then a *light* grey/white dusting from above only (Storm's Gate black-prime-plus-red-spray-from-above Bloodletters use exactly this logic: https://stormsgatecreations.com/2017/08/02/army-painting-bloodletters/).

### 2.3 OSL basics for beginners (the shared ghost-light and the god glows)
- **[B] The 20-minute method (Goonhammer):** around the light source drybrush greys up to pure white (white on the source itself), then paint Contrast Aethermatic Blue over the whole lit area; deepen the outer zone with Drakenhof Nightshade/Incubi Darkness, re-pick raised edges with Baharroth Blue and the source with Blue Horror or Ulthuan Grey (https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-quick-and-easy-osl-the-20-min-methods = https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-quick-and-easy-osl-the-20-min-methods/ ; https://www.goonhammer.com/beer-bristles-episode-1-20-minute-osl-baharroth-blue-and-you). Swap the Contrast for the god colour: Blood Angels Red/Magmadroth Flame (Khorne heat), Doomfire Magenta or Talassar Blue (Tzeentch), Mantis Warriors Green/Tesseract Glow (Nurgle). [colour swaps = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- **[B] Speedpaint/Contrast glaze glow:** glaze a bright transparent (Army Painter Speedpaint Nuclear Sunrise in the source article) softly onto surfaces facing the light — keep it subtle, a hue change rather than a repaint (https://www.goonhammer.com/hobby-102-easy-osl-glow-with-speedpaints).
- Rules that stop OSL going wrong: the source must be the lightest, most saturated thing on the model; light travels in straight lines and does not wrap around limbs; keep the lit radius small on dark models; chalky glow = paint too thick/dry — glaze it back with the transparent colour (https://creativetwilight.com/osl/ ; https://birdwithabrush.com/2022/04/16/how-to-paint-osl-better-than-me/ ; https://www.lightminiatures.com/tutorial-object-source-lighting-osl-and-other-lighting-effects/ ; https://anvilindustry.co.uk/painting-glow-effect).
- **Army ghost-light recipe [B]** (use on eyes, Be'lakor's blade, base cracks, War Dog lenses): Corax White/White Scar dot or line → thin Aethermatic Blue over and slightly around it → re-dot White Scar in the centre → optional Baharroth Blue feathered halo on adjacent black skin by drybrush. 1–3 min per infantry model. [uncited — general hobby knowledge, derived from the Goonhammer method above]

### 2.4 Bloodletters / Khorne flesh (also Flesh Hounds, Bloodcrushers, heralds)

**Reference bright recipes (what your eBay Bloodletters probably are):** Khorne Red base → Mephiston Red drybrush → Carroburg Crimson wash → Mephiston Red drybrush → Evil Sunz Scarlet drybrush + face edge highlights → Jokaero Orange top highlights; Contrast route = Grey Seer primer + Flesh Tearers Red (or Blood Angels Red on Bloodcrusher riders/panels); hellblades wet-blended Mephiston Red → Khorne Red → Abaddon Black, or glazed to a hot tip (Goonhammer HTPE Daemons of Khorne https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-daemons-of-khorne/ ; hellblade heat-glaze focal point, ~1:3 paint:water glazes shrinking toward the tip https://www.jongrantminiatures.co.uk/2023/11/how-i-paint-chaos-daemons-bloodletters.html ; white-zenithal-for-vivid-red speed method https://another-wargaming-blog.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-to-speed-paint-bloodletters.html). Classic Citadel 4-step row: Khorne Red → Agrax Earthshade → Wazdakka Red → Squig Orange (Citadel painting-system chart, https://spikeybits.com/free-pdf-citadels-painting-system-chart-download/) [row contents = uncited — general hobby knowledge].

**Shadow-Khorne, beginner [B] (new/ stripped models, ~25–30 min each in batches of 10):**
1. Prime Chaos Black. 2. Drybrush Mechanicus Standard Grey waist-up; Dawnstone chest-up; Corax White on face, upper arms, hands, sword (2.2 method 3). 3. One generous coat Flesh Tearers Red Contrast over all skin (deep crimson where grey, black where black). 4. Horns/claws: leave black, edge Skavenblight Dinge → Stormvermin Fur. 5. Hellblade as the god-glow: Khorne Red whole blade → Evil Sunz Scarlet outer half → Fire Dragon Bright outer third → Flash Gitz Yellow edge/tip → White Scar tip dot; thin Carroburg Crimson glaze to smooth. 6. Eyes and tongue: army ghost-light OR Flash Gitz Yellow (choose one army-wide). 7. Base, black rim, matte varnish, 'Ardcoat on tongue/blood. Sources for the components: slapchop mechanics https://www.miniaturesofdeath.com/slapchop-technique-how-to-quickly-paint-miniatures/ and https://tabletopdash.com/slapchop-painting-guide/ (≈15 min/model for the greyscale+Contrast core); dark Contrast needs a stronger white drybrush https://warhammerguild.com/painting/contrast-over-zenithal-guide/ ; blade glow https://www.jongrantminiatures.co.uk/2023/11/how-i-paint-chaos-daemons-bloodletters.html. [step assembly and horn colours = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Shadow-Khorne, advanced [A] ("cooling magma" skin, ~60–90 min each):** reverse shading — start the skin bright and hot (Flash Gitz Yellow/Fire Dragon Bright in the deepest muscle recesses), then layer and drybrush progressively darker, cooler colour over the raised flesh (Wazdakka Red → Khorne Red → Khorne Red+Abaddon Black → Corvus Black on the biggest surfaces and all lower limbs) so heat only shows in the cracks; cracks read hot only if the surrounding value drops hard to dark red/black; the black can be nudged up to Dark Reaper on top planes (Prometian Painting molten skin tutorial https://prometianpainting.wordpress.com/2018/05/22/tutorial-molten-skin/ ; their Bloodletters/Bloodthirster done this way https://prometianpainting.wordpress.com/2017/10/15/finished-models-bloodletters/ , https://prometianpainting.wordpress.com/2017/10/21/finished-models-bloodthirster/ ; Wazdakka Red + fluorescent magenta + Lahmian Medium glaze mix for the hot-to-cool gradient, same source).

**Retrofit for already-bright red Bloodletters [B] (~8–10 min each):** (1) shadow-creep glaze-down with Black Templar:medium 1:1, three receding bands (2.2 method 1); (2) one all-over thin Berserker Bloodshade or Carroburg Crimson glaze on the remaining red to deepen and unify differing reds; (3) re-establish the blade glow only on the outer third (Fire Dragon Bright → Flash Gitz Yellow → white tip); (4) ghost-light eyes; (5) rebase, black rim, matte varnish. Purple/crimson shades darken red less starkly than black (https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/652890.page). [sequence = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Flesh Hounds / Bloodcrushers / Juggernauts:** same skin logic; Juggernaut and Bloodcrusher armour in the army trim bronze (3.2) blackened low down; Blood Angels Red Contrast picks out riders/panels fast (https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-daemons-of-khorne/). Collars of Khorne in bronze with ghost-light runes tie hounds to Be'lakor. [collar idea = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 2.5 Tzeentch — Pink & Blue Horrors, Flamers, Screamers (darkening without mud)

**Reference bright recipes:** Contrast Volupus Pink over a pale primer for Pink Horrors, details in Blood Angels Red / Iyanden Yellow, Basilicanum Grey blades, Ultramarines Blue feathers; Blue Horrors in Contrast Ultramarines Blue; Brimstones Iyanden Yellow then a drybrush of Blood Angels Red; Screamer/flame parts Grey Seer → Apothecary White + Ultramarines Blue; classic layered pink = Carroburg Crimson wash → Pink Horror → Emperor's Children, tongues Naggaroth Night + white (Goonhammer HTPE Daemons of Tzeentch https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-daemons-of-tzeentch/ = https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-daemons-of-tzeentch/). Deep-recess shading mix that keeps pink clean: 1 drop Volupus Pink + 1 drop Shyish Purple + 2 drops Contrast Medium into the deepest folds only (Tale of Painters Pink Horror guides https://taleofpainters.com/2023/05/showcase-pink-horrors-of-tzeentch-inc-painting-guides/). Classic Citadel row Screamer Pink → Carroburg Crimson → Pink Horror → Emperor's Children (chart: https://spikeybits.com/free-pdf-citadels-painting-system-chart-download/) [row = uncited — general hobby knowledge].

**The no-mud rule:** never darken pink or blue with brown (Agrax) or with straight black washes over large areas — they grey out and go dirty. Darken *along the hue wheel toward cold*: pink → magenta → violet → indigo-black; blue → indigo → blue-black. In Citadel terms: Pink Horror/Screamer Pink → Doomfire Magenta or Sigvald Burgundy glaze → Shyish Purple/Druchii Violet in the lower third → Black Templar only at the feet. Blue: Ultramarines/Talassar → Leviadon Blue → Drakenhof Nightshade + Kantor Blue → Black Templar feet. Then *re-saturate* the top surfaces with one thin glaze of the pure hue (Volupus Pink / Talassar Blue + medium) so the colour sings again above the shadow. (Hue-shifted shading principle: Vince Venturella HC 122 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N88NtHNmz1Q ; darker-primer/darker-tone Horror variants and magenta+black shading discussed at http://warpstoneflux.blogspot.com/2010/10/painting-scheme-for-plastic-pink-or.html.) [specific Citadel ladder = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Shadow-Tzeentch, beginner [B] (new models, ~25 min each):** black prime → grey/white drybrush confined to arms, hands, faces, flames and crests (Horrors are all arms — perfect) → Volupus Pink Contrast (Pink Horrors) or Talassar/Ultramarines Blue (Blues, Screamer bodies) over everything → Shyish Purple Contrast:medium 1:1 glazed from the waist down → feet Corvus Black → warpflame (hands, mouths, Flamers' heads, Exalted Flamer): Corax White then Aethermatic Blue for the ghost-light army, or Corax White → thin Doomfire Magenta halo → white core if you want a Tzeentch-specific magenta flame → eyes/teeth Corax White. Flamers of Tzeentch: body entirely shadow (black → indigo drybrush Kantor Blue → Thunderhawk Blue on the "skirt" tips), all the colour budget spent on the flames. Screamers: black undersides, colour only on the dorsal ridge and the bioluminescent spots/eyes; the flying stems painted as base (ash grey) not as model. (Component sources as 2.2/2.3; Screamer flame and tongue recipes https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-daemons-of-tzeentch/.) [assembly = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Shadow-Tzeentch, advanced [A] (~60 min per Horror, more for Flamers):** wet-blend or glaze a true vertical gradient on each Horror: Abaddon Black+Naggaroth Night (feet) → Naggaroth Night → Screamer Pink → Pink Horror → Emperor's Children/Fulgrim Pink on fingertips and face, 5–6 glaze passes per transition (glaze technique https://tangibleday.com/how-to-glaze-miniatures-tutorial-for-blending-color/ ; 8 blending techniques overview https://tangibleday.com/8-must-know-paint-blending-techniques-for-miniatures/); flames done as proper OSL casting magenta or ghost-blue onto nearby black skin (2.3). [ladder = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Retrofit for bright pink/blue Horrors [B] (~8 min each):** Shyish Purple:medium 1:1 glaze-down bands (waist/knee/ankle), Black Templar only on feet; one thin Carroburg Crimson (pinks) or Drakenhof Nightshade (blues) all-over glaze to knock back neon highlights; re-brighten fingertips/face with a drybrush of Emperor's Children (pink) or Lothern Blue (blue); repaint every flame/mouth into the army ghost-light; rebase; matte. Brimstone Horrors: leave them bright — they *are* light sources — but paint their base area with a warm-to-ghost glow ring so they read as fires on the ash. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 2.7 Lord of Change (feathers!)

**Reference:** studio-style LoC schemes prime black, base feathers Macragge/Kantor-type blues, purple washes in shadows, sepia on aged feathers, layered or airbrushed blue→purple→white gradients (https://www.adeptusars.com/tutorials/how-to-paint-kairos-fateweaver ; GW-way LoC https://www.belloflostsouls.net/2017/02/painting-lord-change-gw-way.html ; 4-step LoC https://spikeybits.com/2017/02/4-step-lord-of-change-miniature-painting-for-the-rest-of-us.html ; Contrast+ official video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X4AgOix0XH0).

### 3.3 Colchisian script
- **Freehand with paint [A-]:** plan the glyphs, practise on spare shoulder pads; paint the line-work in Black Legion Contrast (flows like ink, opaque), or Black Legion + Flesh Tearers Red for a softer, burnt-in look on red; then re-highlight the red *inside* the enclosed shapes (1:1 Khorne Red + Squig Orange chunky, Squig Orange fine) so runes look engraved (https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/ ; https://paintpad.app/recipes/1425-word-bearers-colchisian-runes ; freehand video "Easy Free Hand Symbols and Script: Word Bearers" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6pPrIuwiNnE ; rune examples thread https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/650076.page).
- **Micron pen [B]:** Sakura Pigma Micron 005 (0.20 mm) / 01 (0.25 mm) pigment ink is archival and waterproof once dry (https://www.jetpens.com/Sakura-Pigma-Micron-Pen-Size-005-0.2-mm-Black/pd/2042 ; https://www.sakuraofamerica.com/product/pigma-micron/). Hobby caveats: 0.1–0.3 mm nibs work on armour plates; let ink dry completely (overnight is safest) before varnish or it bleeds; brush script looks more fluid, pen script more uniform (https://lounge.belloflostsouls.net/archive/index.php/t-1223.html ; https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/747151.page ; https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/354902.page). Practical: pens skate on satin/gloss — write on a matte-varnished surface; seal with a *sprayed or airbrushed* varnish (a brush can smear the line); store pens tip-down; expect to kill a nib every 15–20 models on textured armour. [practical notes = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- **Transfers [B]:** the Forge World/GW **Word Bearers Legion Transfer Sheet** (A4, 627 waterslide transfers: legion icons, numerals, large vehicle badges, banner art, daemonic runes and Colchisian "sacred geometry"; released 18 June 2022; about US$36.50) covers infantry pads and War Dog panels alike (https://miniset.net/sets/gw-99510102012 ; https://www.warbattlegames.com/products/hh-world-bearers-legion-decals-transfer-sheet-warhammer-forge-world-30k-wbgames ; https://www.warhammer.com/en-WW/shop/word-bearers-legion-transfer-sheet-2022). Older 40k WB sheets turn up on eBay. Apply over gloss ('Ardcoat), Micro Set/Micro Sol to snuggle over trim, matte over the top; a thin Khorne Red glaze over a rune decal makes it look painted-in. [application = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- Recommended mix for a beginner: transfers for icons and big vehicle runes; micron pen for scripture lines on parchment, tabards and shin plates; brush freehand only for 3–4 large hero runes.

### 3.4 Parchment, oath-papers, books (the WB "purity seal" equivalents)
Rakarth Flesh (or Zandri Dust) → Agrax Earthshade/Seraphim Sepia → Rakarth Flesh relayer → Pallid Wych Flesh edge; script as broken micron/brush lines in Rhinox Hide or black, first glyph in Khorne Red; wax seals in black or Naggaroth Night → Xereus Purple (not Imperial red, which vanishes on crimson armour). 'Eavy-Metal-style seal reference: https://paintpad.app/recipes/2462-eavy-metal-style-purity-seals. [WB-specific colour choices = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 3.5 Possessed / Gal Vorbak — the bridge between the two halves
- **Goonhammer Gal Vorbak flesh [B]:** Vallejo Game Color Pale Flesh over the red-primed model's flesh areas → Contrast Guilliman Flesh wash → Pale Flesh drybrush → Berserker Bloodshade over all flesh to tie tones and soften the drybrush → optional Blood for the Blood God where flesh erupts through armour (https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-word-bearers-chaos-space-marines/).
- **Cold-flesh option [B]:** flesh basecoats of Rakarth Flesh / Bugman's Glow / Dryad Bark shaded with Drakenhof Nightshade so organic parts sink into the armour (same source) — and, better still for Shadow Legion, the Beard Bunker's Gal Vorbak use a blue-grey flesh that transitions to black toward hands and feet, with Hexwraith Flame for easy OSL (https://www.thebeardbunker.com/2020/11/fortunate-sons-possessed-gal-vorbak.html). **That is literally the daemon "shadow creep" appearing on marines**: paint Possessed mutations with the Be'lakor skin ladder (Corvus Black → Eshin/Mechanicus → Dawnstone), blackening toward claws, with crimson armour plates "floating" on top and ghost-light in the splits. Other Gal Vorbak references: https://legionpaintingstudio.co.uk/2023/12/10/warhammer-40000-gal-vorbak-word-bearers/ ; https://foriegnartisan.wordpress.com/2019/03/15/the-gal-vorbak/ ; video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AWqa0NI-CrI. [the explicit bridge recipe = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### 3.6 Nudging an existing red/black tabletop job toward Word Bearers crimson (no repaint) [B]
1. Clean; matte varnish if the red is glossy (glazes bead on gloss).
2. All-over armour glaze: Berserker Bloodshade or Carroburg Crimson thinned ~1:1 with Lahmian Medium, two coats, wicking pools — purple-crimson shades darken red "less stark than a black wash"; Agrax Earthshade instead if you want warmer, dirtier terracotta (https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/652890.page ; thinning shades with medium for smooth transitions https://agtom.eu/en/content/73-painting-space-marines-miniatures-step-by-step-guide-techniques-and-paints-warhammer-40000).
3. If the red is orange-bright (Evil Sunz/Wild Rider level): one thin Flesh Tearers Red Contrast:medium 1:2 glaze first, then step 2. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
4. Recess-line the panel gaps with Nuln Oil or 1:1 Khorne Red + Rhinox Hide (https://paintpad.app/recipes/1424-word-bearers-armour-2021).
5. Re-establish ONE edge highlight on upward edges only: Wazdakka Red (or Squig Orange sparingly on helm and pauldron tops).
6. Unify trim to the army metal (bronze straight over old gold/silver: Balthasar Gold covers in two coats; over black trim it is one coat + wash).
7. Add script/decals, ghost-light lenses (Corax White → Aethermatic Blue) or keep the canonical green lenses — pick one for the whole contingent.
8. Rebase, black rim, matte, 'Ardcoat lenses.
About 30–40 min per marine; a 10-man squad in two evenings. Richard Gray's red-armour shading/weathering logic is a good visual reference for where the darks should sit: https://richardgraycreations.com/techmarine-red-armour-tutorial-part-2/.

## 4. War Dogs as daemon engines of the host

References: Goonhammer HTPE Chaos Knights (updated 18 Nov 2025; heraldry, weathering, War Dog conversions) https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-chaos-knights/ ; HTPE Imperial Knights (heraldry placement logic the Chaos houses corrupt) https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-paint-everything-imperial-knights/ ; HTPE Heraldry https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-heraldry/ ; quick Chaos Knight "cheat" tutorial https://spikeybits.com/2019/11/cheat-at-painting-your-chaos-knight-tutorial.html ; War Dog videos https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oCWzh_uiKKY , https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s4IJvf5SIOA.

**Heraldry/decals conventions.** The GW Chaos Knights transfer sheet carries the icons of Houses Lucaris, Herpetrax and Khymere plus generic Chaos runes/markings for carapaces and plates (https://bdcosmos.com/products/chaos-knights-transfer-sheet ; https://shop.shopofmagic.com/products/warhammer-40k-chaos-knights-transfer-sheet). Lore split: Iconoclast houses (renegades, defaced Imperial heraldry, trophies, hazard chevrons) vs. Infernal houses (daemon-pacted, Dark Mechanicum, runes, fleshmetal, glowing corruption); lone traitor pilots are Dreadblades (https://1d6chan.miraheze.org/wiki/Chaos_Knight ; https://wahapedia.ru/wh40k10ed/factions/chaos-knights/). Imperial convention puts house colours/crest on one side and personal heraldry on the other, with the tilting shield carrying allegiance; Chaos Knights keep the layout but corrupt the content — so for War Dogs sworn to a Word Bearers host: **tilting plate/one shoulder = Word Bearers icon (book-and-flame / horned daemon head from the WB sheet's large vehicle badges), other shoulder = the Dog's own kill-marks or an eight-pointed star, carapace edge = a band of Colchisian script.** [placement recommendation = uncited — general hobby knowledge built on the cited guides]

**Quick wins, in priority order (each Dog ~2–3 h total) [B]:**
1. **Base** on the army scheme, biggest and most scenic version (fallen rune-stone, candle stubs, obsidian crack with ghost-light) — instantly "theirs".
2. **Trim swap** to the army bronze (Balthasar Gold → Agrax → Sycorax Bronze). Trim is most of what the eye reads on a Knight.
3. **One heraldic panel each** repainted Khorne Red → Nuln Oil recess → Wazdakka Red edge, with a WB transfer, so the Dogs wear the Legion's colours as vassals without a full repaint.
4. **Shadow/soot gradient on the lower legs and feet:** sponge-stipple Corvus Black/Abaddon Black densest at the feet fading out by the knee, then a light drybrush of the base's ash colour (Administratum Grey/Longbeard Grey) on toes — reads simultaneously as battlefield grime and as Be'lakor's shadow climbing the machine (enamel Streaking Grime alternative for [A]: https://aztoyhobby.com/blogs/news/ak-interactive-streaking-grime-explained).
5. **Daemonic glow** in eye lenses, vents, exhaust interiors and weapon coils in the army ghost-light (drybrush white → Aethermatic Blue → white re-dot; 20-min OSL method https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everything-quick-and-easy-osl-the-20-min-methods).
6. **Script and seals:** micron scripture along armour edges, 2–3 parchment strips (green stuff or paper soaked in PVA) in the 3.4 recipe, chains/skull trophies in bronze and bone.
7. Matte varnish the whole thing last so old and new paint share one finish; gloss the lenses.
What goes wrong: decals silvering on matte paint (always gloss under, Micro Sol over, matte after); bronze over old silver looking thin (two coats, or Rhinox Hide undercoat first); overdone soot turning the Dog into a black blob (stop at the knee; keep upper armour clean for contrast). [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

## 5. Basing and display — the strongest unifier

General method for all three schemes (over existing bases): scrape loose material → (optional) glue 1–3 bits of interest → texture paste with an old brush/spatula up to and slightly over the feet edges → dry fully (1–4 h; overnight for crackle) → wash → drybrush → details/glow → tufts (PVA or superglue gel — superglue vapour can frost fresh varnish, so tuft before varnishing or use PVA) → rim two thin coats → varnish with the model (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-paint-bases-quickly/ ; https://tangibleday.com/citadel-technical-paint-astrogranite-review/). [superglue-frosting note = uncited — general hobby knowledge]

### Scheme A — "Ashes of the shrine-world" (pale ash + scorched debris) — RECOMMENDED [B]
- Why: maximum value contrast under near-black daemons and dark crimson marines; neutral, so both cold ghost-light and warm hellblades pop; the fastest and cheapest; hardest to get wrong.
- Recipe (32 mm, ~12 min hands-on): Astrogranite Debris (coarse) patches + Astrogranite (fine) elsewhere → Nuln Oil or Drakenhof Nightshade wash (Drakenhof gives a colder moonlit ash) → heavy drybrush Dawnstone → drybrush Longbeard Grey / Administratum Grey → very light Pallid Wych Flesh or White Scar on top grit → soot: stipple a little Abaddon Black around the daemon's feet so the shadow visibly "leaks" onto the ground → 1–2 burnt tufts (Army Painter/Gamers Grass "burned" or dark 2 mm) and the odd bone/skull (Zandri Dust → Agrax → Ushabti Bone) → rim Abaddon Black (Astrogranite Debris + Drakenhof + Longbeard Grey: https://www.dakkadakka.com/dakkaforum/posts/list/702078.page ; Astrogranite Debris → Dawnstone → Nuln Oil → Longbeard Grey: https://hobbithobbies.com/2017/08/23/quick-easy-cohesive-bases/ ; ash-wastes basing ideas https://paintpad.app/recipes/3715-bases-ash-wastes , https://yaktribe.games/community/threads/ash-wastes-bases.12287/).
- Ghost-light accent (optional, 2 min): score a short crack into the wet paste with a toothpick on ~1 in 3 bases; when painting, run Corax White into it, then Aethermatic Blue, then a Baharroth Blue drybrush on the crack lips. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- Cost for ~70 bases incl. 10 large: 2 × Astrogranite Debris + 2 × Astrogranite (24 ml pots, ≈US$8–10 each) **or** one Vallejo Earth Texture / AK Terrains 200–250 ml tub (≈US$15–20) tinted grey; Nuln Oil/Drakenhof (≈$8) — you own these for the models anyway; Dawnstone + Longbeard Grey (≈$4.55 + $5.50); tufts 2 sheets (≈$7 each); Abaddon Black. **≈US$45–60 with Citadel pots, ≈US$35 with a Vallejo tub.** [prices = uncited — general hobby knowledge, typical 2025–26 US retail]

### Scheme B — "Cracked obsidian, warp-glow beneath" [B+]
- Why: dramatic, on-theme (the ground itself splitting with Be'lakor's un-light or Khorne's heat); the crackle does the work. Risk: crackle thickness is fussy and it must cure overnight; slightly busier under already-dark models — keep the glow to the cracks and dust the plates pale so silhouettes survive.
- Recipe (~15 min hands-on + overnight): paint the bare/old base surface with the glow — for cold ghost-light: White Scar centre lines → Baharroth Blue → Aethermatic Blue edges; for lava: Flash Gitz/Yriel Yellow hottest centres → Troll Slayer Orange → Fire Dragon Bright → Evil Sunz Scarlet at the cool edges → matte varnish the glow layer (helps the crackle release and crack) → Mordant Earth applied THICK (2–3 mm; thicker = bigger cracks; a hairdryer enlarges cracks) → dry overnight → drybrush plates Eshin Grey → Dawnstone → light Administratum Grey; re-touch crack edges with the brightest glow colour → rim black (Mordant Earth lava method: http://endercrafts.blogspot.com/2020/02/lava-base-with-mordant-earth-technical.html ; https://paintpad.app/recipes/5032-volcanic-themed-basing-guide ; https://borgninespainting.blogspot.com/2021/01/tutorial-quick-easy-lava-bases.html ; Goonhammer How to Base Everything: Lava https://www.tabletopbattles.com/how-to-base-everything-the-floor-is-lava/ ; Prometian lava bases https://prometianpainting.wordpress.com/2018/05/30/tutorial-lava-bases/).
- Around existing painted feet: apply Mordant Earth with a sculpting tool, keep it 1 mm clear of toes, and fill that moat afterwards with a thin skim so shrinkage does not pull at the model. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- Cost: Mordant Earth ×4–5 pots for 70 bases (it goes on thick; ≈$8–10 each) ≈ $40–50 + glow paints ≈ $15 → **≈US$55–70.** [uncited prices]

### Scheme C — "Desecrated temple flagstones, candles and script" [B+ / A- for sculpting]
- Why: the most Word-Bearers-flavoured (ritual site), gives literal surfaces for Colchisian script and warm candlelight; best for characters, Be'lakor, War Dogs and the display board. Slowest per base; consider C for heroes/monsters and A for infantry, sharing the same ash colour so they match.
- Recipe (~20–25 min + curing): roll Milliput/green stuff 2 mm thick on the base, wet the roller (green stuff) or dust talc, press with Green Stuff World "Temple" or "Flagstone" rolling pin with even pressure, trim, break a few slabs out and fill the gaps with Astrogranite ash so it matches Scheme A (https://www.greenstuffworld.com/en/modelling-textured-rolling-pins/365-rolling-pin-hobby-roller-temple.html ; https://www.greenstuffworld.com/en/modelling-textured-rolling-pins/698-rolling-pin-flagstone.html ; how-to https://www.fauxhammer.com/tutorials/how-to-make-scenic-bases-with-green-stuff-world-rollers/). Paint stone Mechanicus Standard Grey → Nuln Oil (+ patches of Agrax/Athonian Camoshade for age) → drybrush Dawnstone → Karak Stone → light Screaming Skull (the Paint Pad Be'lakor rock recipe, https://paintpad.app/recipes/3837-belakor-the-dark-master); candles from 1–1.5 mm plastic rod/stretched sprue with a green-stuff drip, Ushabti Bone → Screaming Skull, flame White → Iyanden Yellow → Fire Dragon Bright tip with a tiny warm OSL ring; script chalked onto slabs with micron pen or thinned Pallid Wych Flesh; blood-script option with Blood for the Blood God; rim black. For pre-painted models: sculpt the putty *around* the feet on the existing base (press texture with the flat end of the pin or a textured stamp where the roller cannot reach). [candle/script specifics = uncited — general hobby knowledge]
- Cost: rolling pin ≈ $15–19; Milliput Standard 2–3 packs (≈$8–12 each) or green stuff 36" (≈$20); plastic rod ≈ $5; paints mostly shared → **≈US$55–75.** [uncited prices]

**Movement trays:** 40k daemon hordes (10× 32 mm Bloodletters/Horrors/Plaguebearers) move far faster on 5- or 10-slot 32 mm skirmish trays (laser-cut MDF or 3D-printed; GW also sells Apocalypse Movement Trays for 32 mm). Texture and paint the tray tops in Scheme A so a unit on its tray looks like a mini-diorama; magnetise base undersides (5×1–2 mm N52 disc + steel/rubber-steel sheet in the tray) if you want them to double as transport. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

**Display board that doubles as storage/transport:** an IKEA-style picture-ledge/box frame or a 60×45 cm MDF tray with 25 mm lip; glue self-adhesive ferrous ("rubber steel") sheet over the whole floor; skin it with the Scheme A ash recipe (texture paste over sand/PVA, greys drybrushed), add a Scheme C ruined-shrine corner (foamcore steps clad in rolled-putty flagstones, a toppled column, candle clusters) at the back as Be'lakor's plinth, and 2–3 obsidian crack lines (Scheme B) radiating from it carrying the ghost-light across the board toward the army's front edge. Magnetised bases grip the steel for car transport (lid = a second tray or a "really useful box" of matching footprint); at events the whole tray lifts onto the table as the army display. Build ≈ 8–12 h, ≈ US$60–90 in MDF, steel sheet, foamcore, putty and paste. [uncited — general hobby knowledge]

## 6. Effort plan (beginner-realistic hours)

Cited anchors: quick texture basing ≈5 min brush-time per base (https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-paint-bases-quickly/); slapchop greyscale+Contrast ≈15 min/model (https://www.miniaturesofdeath.com/slapchop-technique-how-to-quickly-paint-miniatures/); a careful HTPE-standard Be'lakor ≈14 h + 9 h base (https://www.goonhammer.com/how-to-paint-everthing-belakor/). Everything else below is [uncited — general hobby knowledge], padded ~30% for a first-timer.

| Job | Per-model | Army total |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Rebase ~60 infantry (Scheme A over existing bases: scrape, paste, wash, 2 drybrushes, soot, tuft, rim) | 12–15 min | 12–15 h |
| (a) Rebase ~10 large/monster bases (more area + 1–2 scenic bits + glow crack) | 30–45 min | 5–7 h |
| (b) Unifying glaze pass: daemon shadow-creep (3 bands + feet) | 5–8 min infantry; 20–40 min monsters; ~2 h LoC | 7–10 h |
| (b) Word Bearers crimson nudge (glaze, recess, one edge, trim swap, lenses) ~15–20 marines | 30–40 min | 8–12 h |
| (b) War Dogs quick-win package ×2 | 2–3 h each | 4–6 h |
| (c) OSL/glow accents (ghost-light eyes + one focal glow per model; blades on Bloodletters) | 3–6 min infantry; 15–30 min monsters | 5–7 h |
| Be'lakor refresh (glaze, re-drybrush, sword/rune glow, bronze, scenic base) | — | 3–5 h |
| Matte varnish everything (rattle-can in batches, or brush-on) + re-gloss | — | 2–3 h |
| (d) NEW 10-model unit from bare plastic to match (clip/clean/build 2–3 h; prime; confined drybrush 1 h; Contrast 1.5 h; glaze-down 1 h; details/horns/metal 2 h; glow 1 h; bases 2 h) | ~60–70 min | 10–13 h |
| **Whole existing lot to "one army"** (rows a–c + Be'lakor + varnish) | | **≈46–65 h ≈ 6–9 weeks at 6–8 h/week** |

**Order of operations (why this order):**
1. Triage, clean, repair, strip the few that need it (Week 1). Decide the three army constants now: base scheme, army light, trim metal. Paint ONE test model of each type (Bloodletter, Horror, Nurgle thing, marine) end-to-end before batch work.
2. Shadow-creep glazes and WB crimson nudge **before** basing (glaze runs onto bases; you will cover that with paste anyway).
3. Metals/trim swaps, script/decals (gloss → decal → later matte).
4. Glow accents last among paint steps (they must stay the cleanest, brightest marks).
5. Rebase everything in 10-model batches (paste one batch while the previous dries).
6. Matte varnish by batch; re-gloss lenses/slime; tufts if not already on.
7. Only now start new units, using the army bible.

**Minimum viable cohesion if time collapses (≈15–18 h total):** Scheme A bases + black rims on everything (≈17 h → do infantry at 8–10 min "good enough" ≈ 10 h), rattle-can matte (2 h), ghost-light eyes on every daemon and lens on every marine/Dog (3 h), shadow-creep only on the front-rank/most-neon units (2–3 h). That alone will read as one deliberate army across a table.
